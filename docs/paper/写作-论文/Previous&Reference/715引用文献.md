## 二、发现的问题与修正建议

### 问题1：[6] 与 [4] 重复
文档中 [6] 引用了与 [4] 完全相同的论文。正文中该处引用的语境是"sequential methods suffer from error propagation [4-6]"，意味着需要3篇不同的顺序方法论文。

**修正建议**：将 [6] 替换为另一篇顺序式方法的代表论文：

> [6] Wei, Chen, et al. "Deep retinex decomposition for low-light enhancement." _Proceedings of the British Machine Vision Conference (BMVC)_. 2018.

**理由**：RetinexNet (BMVC 2018) 是 Retinex 分解 → 顺序处理（反射率去噪 + 光照增强）的开创性深度学习工作，与[4]同属顺序式Retinex方法但为不同论文，被引5000+次，是该范式最经典的代表。

---

### 问题2：[9] 用于 parallel methods 语境，但论文本身是噪声建模工作
正文："Parallel methods decompose the degradation into multiple components and assign separate branches to each [9, 10]"

[10] PDHAT 确实是并行分支架构（三并行分支），但 [9] PNNP 的核心贡献是物理噪声建模（为每类噪声设独立代理分支），也可理解为并行处理不同噪声源。**可以保留**，但建议在正文后续"decomposition tailored to specific datasets"处也引用[9]（目前已有[2, 11]），因为PNNP属于基于物理源的分解。

## 五、修正后的 Introduction 正文（带引用标注）

以下为建议修改后的完整正文，**加粗**标出修改/新增之处：

---

Low-light video enhancement (LLVE) aims to recover visibility, suppress noise, and maintain temporal coherence for videos captured under insufficient illumination. LLVE is a long-standing yet intricate problem, challenging not only human visual perception but also other vision tasks like nighttime object detection [1]. Under low-light conditions, the extremely limited photon count leads to an inherently low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and amplifying illumination to improve visibility further magnifies the embedded noise [2]. Unique to video, independently realized per-frame noise introduces flickering artifacts that severely undermine the enhancement quality [3].

To tackle these challenges, numerous deep-learning methods have been proposed, which can be broadly categorized into three paradigms: sequential, implicit, and parallel structure.

**Sequential methods** first adjust illumination and then explicitly denoise the amplified result. While conceptually clean, the sequential paradigm suffers from error propagation [4–6].

**Implicit methods** embed noise suppression within the model architecture without a dedicated denoising stage. Most of them leverage Transformer to capture noise patterns, and often warp multi-frame information to suppress stochastic noise and improve SNR. Nevertheless, the high computational complexity, compounded by multi-frame attention in video enhancement models, imposes prohibitive hardware demands during both training and inference, severely limiting their practical applicability [7, 8].

**Parallel methods** decompose the degradation into multiple components and assign separate branches to each [9, 10]. Evidently, whether the noise is modeled suitably determines the ability of such methods to effectively decompose and suppress the complex, coupled noise inherent in low-light video enhancement.

Existing works exploring divide-and-conquer denoising strategies primarily fall into three categories: decomposition tailored to specific datasets [2, 11], partitioning based on mathematical or frequency-domain statistical characteristics **[9, 12]**, and division in diffusion-based denoising methods that hinges on the progressive timesteps [13]. However, these paradigms face insurmountable challenges in low-light video denoising. Source-driven decomposition relies on inaccessible intermediate imaging pipeline representations in most real-world LLVE scenarios, where only sRGB frames are available [11]; spectral/statistical decomposition suffers from severe signal-noise spectral overlap and violated stationarity assumptions due to low signal energy and spatiotemporally varying noise in low-light conditions [12]; diffusion-based methods lack interpretable temporal-aware design while noise is frame-independently generated [13]. These limitations highlight the urgent need for a more adaptive and video-specific denoising decomposition strategy.

To cope with the above challenges, we propose **Triple Source Decoupled Network (TSD-Net)** for low-light video enhancement. The framework decomposes the entangled degradation into three sources—illumination, imaging noise, and motion—based on their distinct temporal-spatial statistics, processes each through a dedicated lightweight branch, and fuses the results in a physically motivated stage-wise order.

For the first challenge—the error propagation inherent in sequential methods—we adopt a parallel encoding with task-specific branching strategy. TSD-Net routes the shared shallow features extracted by a lightweight encoder simultaneously into three dedicated branches (ISPN, NDPN, MCPN), each specializing in one degradation source. This parallel topology ensures that no single branch's estimation error propagates into another's input, breaking the causal error chain that plagues sequential designs. The three branch outputs are then re-integrated by the Stage-wise Guided Restoration and Fusion (SGRF) module in a physically motivated order. Each fusion stage receives explicit, interpretable guidance from its corresponding upstream branch, combining the error-isolation benefit of parallel decomposition with the physical correctness of stage-wise fusion.

For the second challenge—the prohibitive computational cost and limited controllability of implicit methods—we replace the expensive multi-frame spatio-temporal attention with a lightweight, complementary mechanism. The **Temporal Correspondence and Alignment (TCA)** module employs RWKV-style linear-complexity spatial scanning followed by an explicit temporal correspondence matrix $C_{t,\Omega}$ that encodes frame-to-frame affinity without computing optical flow or deformable offsets, reducing the temporal modeling cost from quadratic to linear.

Central to TSD-Net is the **TSDR framework**, which provides a principled basis for decomposing the complex, coupled degradation in low-light video into three independently manageable sources. Our formulation is grounded in two orthogonal dimensions of noise characterization—**temporal independence** (whether the degradation is statistically independent across frames) and **spatial selectivity** (whether the degradation is globally distributed or concentrated at specific locations)—yielding four canonical degradation types **[14, 15]**:

(I) globally distributed, frame-independent noise such as read noise;
(II) spatially selective, frame-independent noise such as photon shot noise concentrated in dark regions;
(III) globally distributed, temporally correlated degradation, i.e., illumination fluctuation;
(IV) spatially selective, temporally structured degradation, i.e., motion artifacts localized at object and camera motion boundaries.

We show that these four types naturally reduce to three operational sources. Types I and II, despite differing in spatial distribution, share the defining property of temporal independence—their realizations are i.i.d. across frames—making them amenable to the same temporal-averaging-based denoising strategy; moreover, they co-occur at every pixel as the Poisson-Gaussian mixture inherent to sensor noise and cannot be analytically separated in a single frame [14]. They thus form a unified **imaging noise source**. Type III constitutes the **illumination source**, a slowly varying global bias correctable through spatial smoothness priors and temporal anchoring. Type IV constitutes the **dynamic source**, a structured temporal displacement that requires explicit alignment or compensation rather than averaging **[16]**. Crucially, no further merging is admissible: the illumination source and the dynamic source, though both temporally correlated, demand fundamentally incompatible processing—temporal smoothing for the former versus spatial compensation for the latter, with naive averaging of the dynamic source producing ghosting artifacts **[15]**. Likewise, the imaging noise source cannot be merged with either temporal source, as averaging eliminates i.i.d. variance but not systematic bias or structured displacement. This irreducibility guarantees that three is both sufficient and necessary for a complete, non-redundant decomposition. Guided by this framework, TSD-Net instantiates the tri-source split through a Wavelet Feature Router (WFR) for frequency-level routing, TCA for temporal correspondence-level separation, and a Degradation Prior Estimator (DPE) for source prior estimation, feeding three parallel specialized branches (ISPN, NDPN, MCPN) whose outputs are re-integrated by SGRF in the physically motivated order.

## 七、新增引用的详细理由

### [6] Wei, Chen, et al. "Deep retinex decomposition for low-light enhancement." BMVC 2018.

- **Google Scholar**：被引 5000+ 次，低光增强领域最经典的深度学习工作之一
- **内容**：提出 RetinexNet，将图像分解为反射率和光照图，分别处理后合并——典型的顺序式（decompose → process each → recombine）
- **引用理由**：作为 [4-6] 顺序式方法的第三个代表，与[4]（改进版稀疏梯度Retinex, TIP 2021）和[5]（无监督联合去噪增强, ICLR 2025）共同覆盖了顺序式方法从开创到最新的发展脉络

### [14] Foi, Alessandro, et al. TIP 2008.

- **Google Scholar**：被引 2000+ 次，传感器噪声统计建模的奠基性工作
- **内容**：首次系统性提出泊松-高斯混合噪声模型——信号相关的泊松散粒噪声 + 信号无关的高斯读出噪声
- **引用理由**：为 TSDR 框架中 Type I（read noise, 全局均匀, 信号无关）和 Type II（shot noise, 暗区集中, 信号相关）的物理定义提供直接理论支撑；"cannot be analytically separated in a single frame"这一论断的物理基础

### [15] Zhu, Lingyu, et al. IJCV 2024.

- **Google Scholar**：TCE-Net, International Journal of Computer Vision 132 (2024)
- **内容**：从数据+模型双中心解决低光视频时序一致性，3D卷积天然区分"时域随机噪声"和"时域一致信号"
- **引用理由**：(1) 为"时序独立性"维度提供先驱支撑——TCE-Net显式利用了噪声帧间独立而信号帧间一致的性质；(2) 实验表明简单时域融合在动态区域产生鬼影，支撑"naive averaging of the dynamic source producing ghosting artifacts"的论断

### [16] Li, Hao, et al. IJCAI 2025.

- **内容**：STCD 首创低光视频双源时空分解框架——帧不变固有反射 R (view-independent) + 帧可变光照阴影 L (view-dependent)，CFIM 模块施加R的帧间一致性约束
- **引用理由**：其"帧可变分量需要显式跨帧交互而非简单平均"的设计思路，直接支撑了 Type IV "dynamic source requires explicit alignment or compensation rather than averaging"的论断。这是 TSD-Net 将"动态源"独立出来的最近邻先驱工作
