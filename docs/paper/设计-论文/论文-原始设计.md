# English Version

## 3. Method

Fig. 2 illustrates the overall architecture of the proposed MINS-Net. As shown in Fig. 2(a), MINS-Net is built upon our formulated **Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework, IFND**. Different from existing low-light enhancement frameworks that treat low-light degradation as a single mixed corruption, IFND explicitly decomposes low-light video degradation into two physically different noise components: low-light imaging noise and illumination flicker noise. The former is mainly caused by insufficient photon arrival and sensor readout uncertainty under low illumination, while the latter is induced by temporally varying exposure, automatic exposure adjustment, or external light fluctuation.

Our IFND consists of three main components: an imaging-flicker noise separator, two noise-specific restoration branches, and a lightweight prior-guided reconstruction head. We design a **Multi-frame Imaging-Flicker Noise Separation module, MINS**, to play the role of the noise separator. MINS first constructs illumination-invariant temporal correspondence within local windows, and then derives imaging and flicker priors from temporal inconsistency cues. Guided by these priors, the input features are routed into an **Imaging Noise Processing Network, INPN**, and a **Flicker Noise Suppression Network, FNSN**, which respectively restore spatial low-light imaging corruption and suppress temporal illumination fluctuation. Finally, the prior-guided reconstruction head fuses the restored features and predicts an enhancement residual to produce the final enhanced center frame.

As depicted in Fig. 2(b), the basic unit of MINS is the **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention Block, SD-TMSAB**, which is composed of layer normalization, an illumination-invariant query-key generator, a local-window cross-frame mutual attention module, an entropy-guided prior generation module, and a feed-forward network. Fig. 2(c) shows the details of the proposed SD-TMSA mechanism.

## 3.1 Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework

Given a low-light video clip centered at time \(t\),

\[
\{I_{t-k}, \ldots, I_t, \ldots, I_{t+k}\},
\]

our goal is to estimate an enhanced center frame \(\hat{I}_t\) with improved visibility, reduced noise, and stable temporal appearance. Different from single-image low-light enhancement, low-light video enhancement needs to handle not only spatial corruption inside each frame, but also temporal brightness inconsistency across neighboring frames.

We observe that the dominant degradations in low-light videos can be divided into two categories. The first one is **low-light imaging noise**, denoted as \(\sigma_{img}\), which is caused by the low photon count and sensor readout process. It usually appears as grain noise, color distortion, low contrast, and missing details. The second one is **illumination flicker noise**, denoted as \(\sigma_{flicker}\), which is caused by abrupt exposure changes, automatic exposure adjustment, or unstable light sources. It mainly appears as frame-to-frame brightness fluctuation and temporal inconsistency.

Therefore, we formulate low-light video enhancement as an imaging-flicker noise decoupling problem:

\[
\mathcal{D}_{low} =
\mathcal{D}_{img} +
\mathcal{D}_{flicker},
\]

where \(\mathcal{D}_{img}\) represents spatial low-light imaging degradation, and \(\mathcal{D}_{flicker}\) represents temporal illumination fluctuation. Instead of restoring all corruptions through a single black-box network, IFND explicitly estimates noise-specific priors and performs degradation-specific restoration.

The overall pipeline of IFND can be written as

\[
\hat{I}_t =
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\mathcal{B}_{img}(F_t, P_t^{img}),
\mathcal{B}_{flicker}(F_t, F_{\Omega}, P_t^{flicker}),
P_t^{img},
P_t^{flicker}
\right),
\]

where \(F_t\) and \(F_{\Omega}\) denote the center-frame and neighboring-frame features, respectively. \(P_t^{img}\) and \(P_t^{flicker}\) are the estimated imaging-noise prior and flicker-noise prior. \(\mathcal{B}_{img}\) denotes the imaging noise processing branch, \(\mathcal{B}_{flicker}\) denotes the flicker suppression branch, and \(\mathcal{R}\) denotes the final reconstruction head.

---

## 3.2 Multi-frame Imaging-Flicker Noise Separation

MINS is the core component of the proposed framework. It transforms generic multi-frame features into noise-aware representations that are suitable for branch-specific restoration.

Let \(F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}\) be the feature of the center frame, and let

\[
F_{\Omega} = \{F_i \mid i \in \Omega\}
\]

denote the neighboring-frame features, where

\[
\Omega = \{t-k, \ldots, t-1, t+1, \ldots, t+k\}.
\]

MINS produces four outputs:

\[
F_t^{img}, \quad F_t^{flicker}, \quad C_{t,\Omega}, \quad
\{P_t^{img}, P_t^{flicker}\},
\]

where \(F_t^{img}\) and \(F_t^{flicker}\) are imaging-noise-aware and flicker-noise-aware features. \(C_{t,\Omega}\) is the local temporal correspondence matrix between the center frame and neighboring frames. \(P_t^{img}\) and \(P_t^{flicker}\) are the noise-specific priors used for feature routing and final reconstruction.

---

## 3.2.1 Illumination-Invariant Temporal Correspondence

A key difficulty in low-light video enhancement is that brightness constancy is often violated. Neighboring frames may have similar structures but very different illumination levels because of flicker or exposure changes. Directly computing temporal attention on raw features may therefore confuse illumination variation with structural mismatch.

To address this problem, we construct illumination-invariant query and key tokens. Following the Retinex-inspired intuition, illumination variation is mainly contained in low-frequency components, while structural information is better preserved in residual high-frequency representations. Given a feature \(F\), we first normalize it and remove its local low-frequency component:

\[
\tilde{F} = \text{LN}(F) - \phi(\text{LN}(F)),
\]

where \(\text{LN}(\cdot)\) denotes layer normalization, and \(\phi(\cdot)\) denotes a lightweight low-frequency extractor implemented by local average pooling.

The query, key, and value are then generated as

\[
Q_t = W_q \tilde{F}_t,
\]

\[
K_i = W_k \tilde{F}_i,
\]

\[
V_i = W_v \text{LN}(F_i),
\]

where \(i \in \Omega\). The local-window temporal correspondence is computed by

\[
C_{t,i} =
\text{Softmax}
\left(
\frac{Q_t K_i^\top}{\sqrt{d}}
\right).
\]

This correspondence focuses more on structure-consistent matching and is less affected by frame-to-frame illumination fluctuation.

---

## 3.2.2 Entropy-Guided Imaging-Flicker Prior Generation

The learned correspondence matrix provides an important temporal uncertainty cue. When the temporal correspondence is sharp and reliable, the neighboring frames provide stable support for the center frame. When the correspondence is diffuse or inconsistent, the local region is likely affected by temporal illumination instability or low-light corruption.

We measure this uncertainty using attention entropy:

\[
H_t =
-\sum_j C_{t,\Omega}^{j} \log C_{t,\Omega}^{j}.
\]

The entropy map is then normalized and transformed into a flicker prior:

\[
P_t^{flicker}
=
\sigma
\left(
\text{Conv}(H_t)
\right),
\]

where \(\sigma(\cdot)\) denotes the sigmoid function. The imaging prior is obtained by the complementary relation:

\[
P_t^{img} = 1 - P_t^{flicker}.
\]

Intuitively, \(P_t^{flicker}\) highlights regions that require temporal illumination stabilization, while \(P_t^{img}\) emphasizes regions dominated by spatial low-light imaging corruption.

---

## 3.2.3 Noise-Aware Feature Decoupling

With the estimated priors, the center-frame feature is decomposed into two noise-aware representations:

\[
F_t^{img} = P_t^{img} \odot F_t,
\]

\[
F_t^{flicker} = P_t^{flicker} \odot F_t,
\]

where \(\odot\) denotes element-wise multiplication. This soft routing mechanism does not force a hard binary separation. Instead, it allows each spatial location to adaptively select how much it should rely on imaging-noise restoration and flicker suppression.

For neighboring frames, the correspondence matrix \(C_{t,\Omega}\) is reused to aggregate temporal support under the center-frame coordinate system:

\[
\bar{F}_{\Omega \rightarrow t}
=
C_{t,\Omega} V_{\Omega}.
\]

This design avoids introducing an additional optical-flow-based alignment module and keeps the temporal reasoning consistent with the estimated noise priors.

---

## 3.3 Imaging Noise Processing Network

The Imaging Noise Processing Network, INPN, aims to restore spatial low-light imaging degradation. This branch mainly handles noise, color distortion, low contrast, and missing texture details caused by insufficient illumination and sensor readout uncertainty.

Given the imaging-aware feature \(F_t^{img}\), INPN performs local and non-local restoration to suppress low-light imaging noise:

\[
\hat{F}_t^{img}
=
\mathcal{B}_{img}(F_t^{img}, P_t^{img}).
\]

The imaging prior \(P_t^{img}\) is used to modulate feature responses, so that the network can pay more attention to regions dominated by low-light imaging noise. Similar to the corruption restorer in Retinexformer, this branch is responsible for recovering the hidden corruption after the low-light feature has been enhanced.

---

## 3.4 Flicker Noise Suppression Network

The Flicker Noise Suppression Network, FNSN, aims to suppress temporal illumination fluctuation. Different from INPN, this branch explicitly uses neighboring frames to estimate a temporally stable illumination reference.

Given the flicker-aware center feature \(F_t^{flicker}\), neighboring features \(F_{\Omega}\), and correspondence matrix \(C_{t,\Omega}\), FNSN first aggregates temporally aligned support:

\[
\bar{F}_{\Omega \rightarrow t}^{flicker}
=
C_{t,\Omega} F_{\Omega}.
\]

Then the flicker-restored feature is computed as

\[
\hat{F}_t^{flicker}
=
\mathcal{B}_{flicker}
\left(
F_t^{flicker},
\bar{F}_{\Omega \rightarrow t}^{flicker},
P_t^{flicker}
\right).
\]

This branch focuses on stabilizing frame-to-frame exposure and suppressing illumination flicker, while preserving the structural content of the center frame.

---

## 3.5 Prior-Guided Final Reconstruction

After obtaining the restored imaging feature \(\hat{F}_t^{img}\) and flicker-stabilized feature \(\hat{F}_t^{flicker}\), we fuse them through a lightweight prior-guided reconstruction head:

\[
\Delta I_t
=
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\hat{F}_t^{img},
\hat{F}_t^{flicker},
P_t^{img},
P_t^{flicker}
\right).
\]

The final enhanced result is produced by residual learning:

\[
\hat{I}_t = I_t + \Delta I_t.
\]

We intentionally keep the reconstruction head lightweight, because the main contribution of MINS-Net lies in the imaging-flicker noise decoupling and noise-specific temporal restoration.

图 2 展示了本文提出的 MINS-Net 的整体结构。如图 2(a) 所示，MINS-Net 基于本文构建的 **Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework，IFND**，即 **成像-闪烁噪声解耦框架**。不同于现有低光增强方法通常将低光退化视为单一混合噪声，IFND 显式地将低光视频退化划分为两类具有不同物理来源的噪声：低光成像噪声和光照闪烁噪声。前者主要由低照度下光子数量不足和传感器读出不确定性引起，后者则主要来源于自动曝光变化、外部光源频闪或跨帧光照波动。

IFND 由三个主要部分组成：成像-闪烁噪声分离模块、两个噪声特定恢复分支，以及轻量级先验引导重建头。本文设计了 **Multi-frame Imaging-Flicker Noise Separation module，MINS** 作为核心噪声分离模块。MINS 首先在局部窗口内构建光照不变的时序对应关系，然后根据时序不一致性估计成像噪声先验和闪烁噪声先验。在这些先验的引导下，输入特征被分别送入 **Imaging Noise Processing Network，INPN** 和 **Flicker Noise Suppression Network，FNSN**。其中，INPN 用于恢复低光成像噪声导致的空间退化，FNSN 用于抑制光照闪烁导致的时序亮度波动。最后，先验引导重建头融合两个恢复分支的输出，并预测增强补偿量，得到最终增强后的中心帧。

如图 2(b) 所示，MINS 的基本单元是 **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention Block，SD-TMSAB**。该模块由层归一化、光照不变 Query-Key 生成器、局部窗口跨帧互注意力模块、熵引导先验生成模块以及前馈网络组成。图 2(c) 展示了所提出的 SD-TMSA 机制的具体结构。

## 3.1 成像-闪烁噪声解耦框架

给定以第 \(t\) 帧为中心的低光视频片段：

\[
\{I_{t-k}, \ldots, I_t, \ldots, I_{t+k}\},
\]

我们的目标是估计增强后的中心帧 \(\hat{I}_t\)，使其同时具有更好的可见性、更少的噪声以及更稳定的时序表现。与单帧低光图像增强不同，低光视频增强不仅需要处理每一帧内部的空间退化，还需要处理相邻帧之间的亮度不一致问题。

我们观察到，低光视频中的主要退化可以划分为两类。第一类是 **低光成像噪声**，记为 \(\sigma_{img}\)。它主要由低光照条件下光子数量不足和传感器读出噪声引起，通常表现为颗粒噪声、颜色失真、对比度不足和细节缺失。第二类是 **光照闪烁噪声**，记为 \(\sigma_{flicker}\)。它主要由自动曝光切换、外部灯光频闪或光照不稳定引起，通常表现为跨帧亮度跳变和时序不一致。

因此，我们将低光视频增强重新表述为成像-闪烁噪声解耦问题：

\[
\mathcal{D}_{low} =
\mathcal{D}_{img} +
\mathcal{D}_{flicker},
\]

其中，\(\mathcal{D}_{img}\) 表示空间低光成像退化，\(\mathcal{D}_{flicker}\) 表示时序光照波动退化。与单一黑盒恢复网络不同，IFND 显式估计噪声特定先验，并进行分支特定恢复。

IFND 的整体流程可以表示为：

\[
\hat{I}_t =
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\mathcal{B}_{img}(F_t, P_t^{img}),
\mathcal{B}_{flicker}(F_t, F_{\Omega}, P_t^{flicker}),
P_t^{img},
P_t^{flicker}
\right),
\]

其中，\(F_t\) 和 \(F_{\Omega}\) 分别表示中心帧特征和邻帧特征。\(P_t^{img}\) 和 \(P_t^{flicker}\) 分别表示成像噪声先验和闪烁噪声先验。\(\mathcal{B}_{img}\) 表示成像噪声处理分支，\(\mathcal{B}_{flicker}\) 表示闪烁噪声抑制分支，\(\mathcal{R}\) 表示最终重建头。

---

## 3.2 多帧成像-闪烁噪声分离

MINS 是本文方法的核心模块。它将普通的多帧特征转换为噪声感知特征表示，从而为后续分支特定恢复提供基础。

设 \(F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}\) 表示中心帧特征，邻帧特征集合表示为：

\[
F_{\Omega} = \{F_i \mid i \in \Omega\},
\]

其中：

\[
\Omega = \{t-k, \ldots, t-1, t+1, \ldots, t+k\}.
\]

MINS 输出四类结果：

\[
F_t^{img}, \quad F_t^{flicker}, \quad C_{t,\Omega}, \quad
\{P_t^{img}, P_t^{flicker}\}.
\]

其中，\(F_t^{img}\) 和 \(F_t^{flicker}\) 分别表示成像噪声感知特征和闪烁噪声感知特征。\(C_{t,\Omega}\) 表示中心帧与邻帧之间的局部时序对应矩阵。\(P_t^{img}\) 和 \(P_t^{flicker}\) 分别表示两类噪声先验，用于特征路由和最终重建。

---

## 3.2.1 光照不变时序对应关系

低光视频增强的一个核心难点在于亮度恒定假设往往不成立。相邻帧可能具有相似的结构内容，但由于闪烁或曝光变化而具有明显不同的亮度。若直接在原始特征上计算时序注意力，网络容易将光照变化误认为结构错配。

为了解决这一问题，本文构建光照不变的 Query 和 Key。根据 Retinex 思想，光照变化主要存在于低频成分中，而可靠的结构匹配更依赖于残差结构信息。对于特征 \(F\)，我们首先对其进行归一化，并去除局部低频成分：

\[
\tilde{F} = \text{LN}(F) - \phi(\text{LN}(F)),
\]

其中，\(\text{LN}(\cdot)\) 表示层归一化，\(\phi(\cdot)\) 表示由局部平均池化实现的轻量级低频提取器。

随后，Query、Key 和 Value 分别表示为：

\[
Q_t = W_q \tilde{F}_t,
\]

\[
K_i = W_k \tilde{F}_i,
\]

\[
V_i = W_v \text{LN}(F_i),
\]

其中 \(i \in \Omega\)。局部窗口内的时序对应关系计算为：

\[
C_{t,i} =
\text{Softmax}
\left(
\frac{Q_t K_i^\top}{\sqrt{d}}
\right).
\]

该对应关系更加关注结构一致性匹配，从而降低跨帧光照波动对时序建模的干扰。

---

## 3.2.2 熵引导的成像-闪烁先验生成

学习得到的时序对应矩阵提供了重要的不确定性信息。当时序对应关系集中且清晰时，说明邻帧可以为中心帧提供稳定的时序支持；当对应关系分散或不稳定时，说明该区域更可能受到光照闪烁或低光退化影响。

本文使用注意力熵度量这种不确定性：

\[
H_t =
-\sum_j C_{t,\Omega}^{j} \log C_{t,\Omega}^{j}.
\]

随后，将熵图归一化并映射为闪烁噪声先验：

\[
P_t^{flicker}
=
\sigma
\left(
\text{Conv}(H_t)
\right),
\]

其中，\(\sigma(\cdot)\) 表示 Sigmoid 函数。成像噪声先验则通过互补关系得到：

\[
P_t^{img} = 1 - P_t^{flicker}.
\]

直观上，\(P_t^{flicker}\) 用于强调需要进行时序光照稳定的区域，而 \(P_t^{img}\) 用于强调主要受低光成像噪声影响的区域。

---

## 3.2.3 噪声感知特征解耦

根据估计得到的先验，中心帧特征被解耦为两类噪声感知表示：

\[
F_t^{img} = P_t^{img} \odot F_t,
\]

\[
F_t^{flicker} = P_t^{flicker} \odot F_t,
\]

其中，\(\odot\) 表示逐元素乘法。该软路由机制并不强制进行二值化分离，而是允许每个空间位置自适应地选择更多依赖成像噪声恢复还是闪烁噪声抑制。

对于邻帧特征，本文复用时序对应矩阵 \(C_{t,\Omega}\)，将邻帧信息聚合到中心帧坐标系下：

\[
\bar{F}_{\Omega \rightarrow t}
=
C_{t,\Omega} V_{\Omega}.
\]

该设计避免了额外引入光流估计或显式对齐模块，同时保证了时序推理与噪声先验估计之间的一致性。

---

## 3.3 成像噪声处理网络

成像噪声处理网络 INPN 用于恢复低光成像噪声导致的空间退化。该分支主要处理由光照不足和传感器读出不确定性引起的噪声、颜色失真、低对比度和纹理缺失。

给定成像噪声感知特征 \(F_t^{img}\)，INPN 对其进行局部和非局部恢复：

\[
\hat{F}_t^{img}
=
\mathcal{B}_{img}(F_t^{img}, P_t^{img}).
\]

其中，成像噪声先验 \(P_t^{img}\) 用于调制特征响应，使网络更加关注低光成像噪声明显的区域。类似于 Retinexformer 中的 corruption restorer，该分支主要负责恢复低光增强过程中隐藏或放大的空间退化。

---

## 3.4 闪烁噪声抑制网络

闪烁噪声抑制网络 FNSN 用于抑制时序光照波动。与 INPN 不同，该分支显式利用邻帧信息估计更加稳定的时序光照参考。

给定闪烁噪声感知中心特征 \(F_t^{flicker}\)、邻帧特征 \(F_{\Omega}\) 以及时序对应矩阵 \(C_{t,\Omega}\)，FNSN 首先聚合对齐后的时序支持：

\[
\bar{F}_{\Omega \rightarrow t}^{flicker}
=
C_{t,\Omega} F_{\Omega}.
\]

随后，闪烁抑制后的特征表示为：

\[
\hat{F}_t^{flicker}
=
\mathcal{B}_{flicker}
\left(
F_t^{flicker},
\bar{F}_{\Omega \rightarrow t}^{flicker},
P_t^{flicker}
\right).
\]

该分支主要负责稳定跨帧曝光、抑制亮度闪烁，同时保留中心帧的结构内容。

---

## 3.5 先验引导最终重建

在获得成像噪声恢复特征 \(\hat{F}_t^{img}\) 和闪烁稳定特征 \(\hat{F}_t^{flicker}\) 后，本文使用轻量级先验引导重建头进行融合：

\[
\Delta I_t
=
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\hat{F}_t^{img},
\hat{F}_t^{flicker},
P_t^{img},
P_t^{flicker}
\right).
\]

最终增强结果通过残差学习得到：

\[
\hat{I}_t = I_t + \Delta I_t.
\]

我们有意保持最终重建头的轻量化，因为本文的主要创新集中在成像-闪烁噪声解耦以及噪声特定的时序恢复机制上。

# English Version

## 3. Method

Our **MINS-Net** is based on our formulated **Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework, IFND**. IFND is designed for low-light video enhancement and explicitly decomposes low-light video degradation into imaging-related corruption and flicker-related temporal fluctuation. Specifically, IFND consists of a shared feature extractor, a Motion-Illumination Noise Separation module, two source-specific restoration branches, and a lightweight prior-guided reconstruction head. We design the **Motion-Illumination Noise Separation module, MINS**, to play the role of the source separator. MINS first constructs illumination-invariant temporal correspondence within local windows, and then derives source-aware priors from the uncertainty of temporal attention. Guided by these priors, an **Illumination-Source Processing Network, ISPN**, is employed to suppress illumination flicker and stabilize exposure, while a **Motion-Source Processing Network, MSPN**, reuses the correspondence matrix to align neighboring features and restore motion-related degradation. The restored source features are finally fused by the reconstruction head to predict the enhanced center frame.

The basic unit of MINS is the **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention Block, SD-TMSAB**. It is composed of layer normalization, an illumination-invariant query-key generator, a cross-frame mutual attention module, an entropy-guided source prior generator, and a feed-forward network. The core component of SD-TMSAB is **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention, SD-TMSA**, which modifies conventional temporal mutual attention by suppressing low-frequency illumination bias in query-key matching and by explicitly separating motion-sensitive and illumination-sensitive representations.

## 3.1 Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework

Given a low-light video clip centered at time \(t\),

\[
\mathcal{I}_t=\{I_{t-k},\ldots,I_t,\ldots,I_{t+k}\},
\]

our goal is to estimate an enhanced center frame

\[
\hat{I}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}
\]

with improved visibility, reduced noise, and temporally stable appearance. Compared with low-light image enhancement, low-light video enhancement is more challenging because the degradation is not only spatial but also temporal. Neighboring frames provide complementary information, but they may suffer from brightness fluctuation, motion blur, sensor noise, and cross-frame misalignment.

We formulate low-light video enhancement as an imaging-flicker noise decoupling problem. In real low-light videos, the degradation can be roughly divided into two physically different sources. The first one is **low-light imaging noise**, denoted as \(\sigma_{img}\), which is mainly caused by insufficient photon arrival, shot noise, read noise, color distortion, and motion blur hidden in dark regions. The second one is **illumination flicker noise**, denoted as \(\sigma_{flicker}\), which is mainly caused by automatic exposure variation, unstable light sources, and frame-to-frame illumination fluctuation.

Therefore, the observed low-light degradation can be formulated as

\[
\mathcal{D}_{low}
=
\mathcal{D}_{img}
+
\mathcal{D}_{flicker},
\]

where \(\mathcal{D}_{img}\) denotes imaging-related degradation and \(\mathcal{D}_{flicker}\) denotes temporal illumination fluctuation. Instead of restoring all degradations with a single black-box network, IFND explicitly estimates source priors and conducts branch-specific restoration.

The overall process of IFND can be written as

\[
F_t, F_{\Omega}
=
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{I}_t),
\]

\[
F_t^{mot}, F_t^{illu}, C_{t,\Omega}, P_t^{mot}, P_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{M}(F_t,F_{\Omega}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}(F_t^{illu},F_{\Omega},P_t^{illu}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{mot}
=
\mathcal{B}_{mot}(F_t^{mot},F_{\Omega},C_{t,\Omega},P_t^{mot}),
\]

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
\mathcal{R}(\hat{F}_t^{illu},\hat{F}_t^{mot},P_t^{illu},P_t^{mot}),
\]

where \(\mathcal{E}\) denotes the shared feature extractor, \(\mathcal{M}\) denotes the proposed MINS module, \(\mathcal{B}_{illu}\) and \(\mathcal{B}_{mot}\) denote ISPN and MSPN respectively, and \(\mathcal{R}\) denotes the final reconstruction head. \(F_t\) is the center-frame feature and \(F_{\Omega}\) is the neighboring-frame feature set.

---

## 3.2 Motion-Illumination Noise Separation Module

MINS is the core component of MINS-Net. It transforms generic multi-frame features into source-aware representations that are suitable for illumination-source processing and motion-source processing.

Let

\[
F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}
\]

denote the center-frame feature, and

\[
F_{\Omega}=\{F_i\mid i\in\Omega\}
\]

denote the neighboring-frame features, where

\[
\Omega=\{t-k,\ldots,t-1,t+1,\ldots,t+k\}.
\]

MINS produces four types of outputs:

\[
F_t^{mot},\quad F_t^{illu},\quad C_{t,\Omega},\quad \{P_t^{mot},P_t^{illu}\}.
\]

Here, \(F_t^{mot}\) and \(F_t^{illu}\) are the motion-source and illumination-source features. \(C_{t,\Omega}\) is the temporal correspondence matrix from the center frame to neighboring frames. \(P_t^{mot}\) and \(P_t^{illu}\) are the estimated source priors, which indicate the motion-sensitive and illumination-sensitive regions respectively.

---

## 3.2.1 Illumination-Invariant Local-Window Correspondence

Conventional temporal mutual attention usually assumes that cross-frame differences mainly come from motion. However, in low-light videos, a large part of the cross-frame variation is caused by illumination jump or flicker rather than pure motion. Directly computing query-key similarity on original features may therefore lead to unreliable temporal matching.

To address this issue, we construct illumination-invariant query and key tokens. Following the Retinex-inspired observation that illumination variation is mainly carried by smooth low-frequency components, we remove the local low-frequency component before generating query and key.

Given a feature \(F\), we first apply layer normalization and then extract its low-frequency component using a lightweight local average pooling operator \(\phi(\cdot)\):

\[
F^{n}=\text{LN}(F),
\]

\[
F^{r}=F^{n}-\phi(F^{n}),
\]

where \(F^{r}\) denotes the illumination-suppressed residual feature. The query and key are generated from the residual features:

\[
Q_t=W_q F_t^{r},
\]

\[
K_i=W_k F_i^{r}, \quad i\in\Omega.
\]

The value is generated from the normalized feature:

\[
V_i=W_v F_i^{n}.
\]

Then, we compute local-window temporal correspondence as

\[
C_{t,i}
=
\text{Softmax}
\left(
\frac{Q_tK_i^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}
\right),
\quad i\in\Omega,
\]

where \(d\) is the channel dimension of each attention head. The correspondence matrix \(C_{t,i}\) represents the matching relationship between the center-frame tokens and neighboring-frame tokens. Since the query-key matching is performed on illumination-suppressed residual features, the correspondence is more robust to brightness fluctuation and flicker.

The neighboring temporal support is aggregated by

\[
A_{t,i}=C_{t,i}V_i,
\]

and the multi-frame support is obtained as

\[
A_{t,\Omega}
=
\text{Agg}\left(\{A_{t,i}\mid i\in\Omega\}\right),
\]

where \(\text{Agg}(\cdot)\) denotes temporal aggregation, such as concatenation followed by linear projection or weighted summation.

---

## 3.2.2 Residual Correction

Although neighboring frames provide useful temporal evidence, the center frame should remain the semantic anchor of the reconstruction. Therefore, we introduce a residual correction mechanism to combine the temporal support with the center-frame feature:

\[
Y_t
=
F_t
+
W_o A_{t,\Omega},
\]

where \(W_o\) is an output projection layer. This residual design preserves the center-frame structure and introduces complementary information from neighboring frames. It also avoids over-smoothing caused by excessive temporal fusion.

---

## 3.2.3 Entropy-Guided Source Prior Generation

The temporal correspondence matrix not only provides alignment information but also contains uncertainty cues. If the attention distribution is sharp, the temporal matching is confident and stable. If the attention distribution is diffuse, the current position is likely to suffer from motion ambiguity, blur, occlusion, or severe temporal inconsistency.

We measure this uncertainty using attention entropy:

\[
H_t
=
-
\sum_j
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}
\log
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}.
\]

The entropy map is normalized and mapped into a motion-source prior:

\[
P_t^{mot}
=
\sigma
\left(
\text{Conv}(H_t)
\right),
\]

where \(\sigma(\cdot)\) denotes the sigmoid function. The illumination-source prior is obtained by the complementary relation:

\[
P_t^{illu}
=
1-P_t^{mot}.
\]

Intuitively, \(P_t^{mot}\) highlights regions with unreliable temporal correspondence, such as motion blur, occlusion, and misalignment. \(P_t^{illu}\) emphasizes regions where illumination-source processing is more important, such as exposure fluctuation and flicker-dominated areas.

---

## 3.2.4 Source Decoupling and Backward Prior Projection

With the estimated source priors, we decompose the corrected center-frame feature \(Y_t\) into two source-aware components:

\[
F_t^{mot}
=
P_t^{mot}
\odot
Y_t,
\]

\[
F_t^{illu}
=
P_t^{illu}
\odot
Y_t,
\]

where \(\odot\) denotes element-wise multiplication. This soft routing strategy does not enforce hard separation. Instead, it allows each spatial position to adaptively select the degree of motion-source and illumination-source processing.

To preserve multi-frame source-aware reasoning, we further project the center priors back to neighboring frames using the transposed correspondence matrix:

\[
P_i^{mot}
=
C_{t,i}^{\top}P_t^{mot},
\]

\[
P_i^{illu}
=
C_{t,i}^{\top}P_t^{illu},
\quad i\in\Omega.
\]

This backward prior projection keeps neighboring frames involved in the source-decoupled restoration process, rather than collapsing all temporal information into the center frame too early.

---

## 3.3 Illumination-Source Processing Network

ISPN aims to suppress illumination flicker and stabilize cross-frame exposure. The key idea is to estimate a shared illumination reference from neighboring frames and then normalize the center illumination feature with this reference.

Given the illumination-source feature \(F_t^{illu}\), the neighboring features \(F_{\Omega}\), and the projected illumination priors \(\{P_i^{illu}\}\), ISPN first extracts neighbor-only illumination evidence:

\[
G_{\Omega}^{illu}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{
P_i^{illu}\odot F_i
\mid
i\in\Omega
\}
\right).
\]

Then, a shared illumination representation is estimated as

\[
L_t^{ref}
=
\mathcal{G}_{illu}(G_{\Omega}^{illu}),
\]

where \(\mathcal{G}_{illu}\) denotes a lightweight illumination estimation network. The center illumination feature is corrected by

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}
\left(
F_t^{illu},
L_t^{ref},
P_t^{illu}
\right).
\]

Different from simple temporal feature fusion, ISPN uses neighboring frames mainly to estimate stable illumination evidence. This design reduces frame-to-frame brightness fluctuation while preserving the structure of the center frame.

---

## 3.4 Motion-Source Processing Network

MSPN is responsible for restoring motion-related degradation, including motion blur, ghosting, and misalignment. Instead of introducing an additional optical-flow estimation module, MSPN directly reuses the correspondence matrix generated by MINS.

For each neighboring frame, we align its motion-source feature to the center-frame coordinate system:

\[
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}^{mot}
=
C_{t,i}
\left(
P_i^{mot}\odot F_i
\right).
\]

The aligned neighboring features are then adaptively fused:

\[
G_t^{mot}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}^{mot}
\mid
i\in\Omega
\}
\right).
\]

Finally, MSPN restores the motion-source feature as

\[
\hat{F}_t^{mot}
=
\mathcal{B}_{mot}
\left(
F_t^{mot},
G_t^{mot},
P_t^{mot}
\right).
\]

Since MSPN reuses the illumination-invariant correspondence matrix, the alignment process is less affected by flicker and exposure changes. This makes the motion restoration more robust in low-light videos.

---

## 3.5 Prior-Guided Final Reconstruction

After obtaining the restored illumination-source feature \(\hat{F}_t^{illu}\) and motion-source feature \(\hat{F}_t^{mot}\), we use a lightweight reconstruction head to produce the enhanced center frame. The reconstruction head is guided by the source priors:

\[
\Delta I_t
=
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\hat{F}_t^{illu},
\hat{F}_t^{mot},
P_t^{illu},
P_t^{mot}
\right).
\]

The final enhanced result is generated by residual learning:

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
I_t
+
\Delta I_t.
\]

We intentionally keep the reconstruction head lightweight, because the main contribution of MINS-Net lies in the proposed Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework and the source-decoupled temporal reasoning mechanism.

---

## 3.6 Training Objective

For the first implementation stage, we adopt a concise objective consisting of reconstruction loss and prior regularization. Given the ground-truth normal-light frame \(I_t^{gt}\), the reconstruction loss is

\[
\mathcal{L}_{rec}
=
\left\|
\hat{I}_t
-
I_t^{gt}
\right\|_1.
\]

To encourage spatially smooth and physically meaningful priors, we apply a total variation regularization:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{prior}
=
\left\|
\nabla P_t^{mot}
\right\|_1
+
\left\|
\nabla P_t^{illu}
\right\|_1.
\]

The total objective is

\[
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{rec}
+
\lambda
\mathcal{L}_{prior},
\]

where \(\lambda\) is a balancing coefficient.

## 3. 方法

本文提出的 **MINS-Net** 基于我们构建的 **Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework，IFND**，即 **成像-闪烁噪声解耦框架**。IFND 面向低光视频增强任务，显式地将低光视频退化解耦为成像相关退化和闪烁相关时序波动。具体而言，IFND 由共享特征提取器、运动-光照噪声分离模块、两个源特定恢复分支以及轻量级先验引导重建头组成。我们设计了 **Motion-Illumination Noise Separation module，MINS** 作为核心源分离模块。MINS 首先在局部窗口内构建光照不变的时序对应关系，然后根据时序注意力的不确定性生成源感知先验。在这些先验的引导下，**Illumination-Source Processing Network，ISPN** 用于抑制光照闪烁并稳定跨帧曝光，**Motion-Source Processing Network，MSPN** 则复用时序对应矩阵对齐邻帧特征，并恢复运动相关退化。最后，两个恢复后的源特征由重建头进行融合，得到增强后的中心帧。

MINS 的基本单元是 **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention Block，SD-TMSAB**。该模块由层归一化、光照不变 Query-Key 生成器、跨帧互注意力模块、熵引导源先验生成模块以及前馈网络组成。SD-TMSAB 的核心是 **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention，SD-TMSA**。与传统时序互注意力不同，SD-TMSA 在 Query-Key 匹配阶段抑制低频光照偏置，并显式分离运动敏感表示和光照敏感表示。

---

## 3.1 成像-闪烁噪声解耦框架

给定以第 \(t\) 帧为中心的低光视频片段：

\[
\mathcal{I}_t=\{I_{t-k},\ldots,I_t,\ldots,I_{t+k}\},
\]

我们的目标是估计增强后的中心帧：

\[
\hat{I}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3},
\]

使其具有更好的可见性、更少的噪声以及更稳定的时序表现。相比低光图像增强，低光视频增强更加困难，因为其退化不仅存在于单帧空间维度，也存在于跨帧时间维度。邻帧虽然能够提供冗余结构信息，但同时也会引入亮度波动、运动模糊、传感器噪声以及跨帧错位等问题。

我们将低光视频增强表述为一个成像-闪烁噪声解耦问题。在真实低光视频中，主要退化可以划分为两类具有不同物理来源的噪声。第一类是 **低光成像噪声**，记为 \(\sigma_{img}\)。它主要由低照度下光子数量不足、光子散粒噪声、传感器读出噪声、颜色失真以及暗光区域中的运动模糊引起。第二类是 **光照闪烁噪声**，记为 \(\sigma_{flicker}\)。它主要来源于自动曝光变化、不稳定光源以及帧间亮度波动。

因此，低光视频中的观测退化可以表示为：

\[
\mathcal{D}_{low}
=
\mathcal{D}_{img}
+
\mathcal{D}_{flicker},
\]

其中，\(\mathcal{D}_{img}\) 表示成像相关退化，\(\mathcal{D}_{flicker}\) 表示时序光照波动退化。与使用单一黑盒网络恢复所有退化不同，IFND 显式估计源特定先验，并进行分支特定恢复。

IFND 的整体流程可以表示为：

\[
F_t, F_{\Omega}
=
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{I}_t),
\]

\[
F_t^{mot}, F_t^{illu}, C_{t,\Omega}, P_t^{mot}, P_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{M}(F_t,F_{\Omega}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}(F_t^{illu},F_{\Omega},P_t^{illu}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{mot}
=
\mathcal{B}_{mot}(F_t^{mot},F_{\Omega},C_{t,\Omega},P_t^{mot}),
\]

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
\mathcal{R}(\hat{F}_t^{illu},\hat{F}_t^{mot},P_t^{illu},P_t^{mot}),
\]

其中，\(\mathcal{E}\) 表示共享特征提取器，\(\mathcal{M}\) 表示所提出的 MINS 模块，\(\mathcal{B}_{illu}\) 和 \(\mathcal{B}_{mot}\) 分别表示 ISPN 和 MSPN，\(\mathcal{R}\) 表示最终重建头。\(F_t\) 表示中心帧特征，\(F_{\Omega}\) 表示邻帧特征集合。

---

## 3.2 运动-光照噪声分离模块

MINS 是 MINS-Net 的核心组成部分。它将普通的多帧特征转换为源感知特征表示，从而为后续光照源处理和运动源处理提供基础。

设

\[
F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}
\]

表示中心帧特征，

\[
F_{\Omega}=\{F_i\mid i\in\Omega\}
\]

表示邻帧特征集合，其中

\[
\Omega=\{t-k,\ldots,t-1,t+1,\ldots,t+k\}.
\]

MINS 输出四类结果：

\[
F_t^{mot},\quad F_t^{illu},\quad C_{t,\Omega},\quad \{P_t^{mot},P_t^{illu}\}.
\]

其中，\(F_t^{mot}\) 和 \(F_t^{illu}\) 分别表示运动源特征和光照源特征。\(C_{t,\Omega}\) 表示中心帧到邻帧的时序对应矩阵。\(P_t^{mot}\) 和 \(P_t^{illu}\) 分别表示估计得到的源先验，用于指示运动敏感区域和光照敏感区域。

---

## 3.2.1 光照不变局部窗口时序对应

传统时序互注意力通常默认跨帧差异主要来自运动。然而，在低光视频中，相邻帧之间的大量变化实际上来源于 illumination jump 或 flicker，而不是纯粹的运动位移。如果直接在原始特征上计算 Query-Key 相似度，网络容易将光照变化误认为结构错配，从而导致不可靠的时序匹配。

为了解决这一问题，我们构建光照不变的 Query 和 Key。根据 Retinex 启发式观察，光照变化主要存在于平滑的低频分量中，而稳定的结构匹配更依赖于去除低频光照后的残差信息。因此，在生成 Query 和 Key 之前，我们先去除局部低频光照成分。

给定特征 \(F\)，首先对其进行层归一化，并使用轻量级局部平均池化算子 \(\phi(\cdot)\) 提取低频成分：

\[
F^{n}=\text{LN}(F),
\]

\[
F^{r}=F^{n}-\phi(F^{n}),
\]

其中，\(F^{r}\) 表示抑制光照后的残差特征。随后，Query 和 Key 由残差特征生成：

\[
Q_t=W_q F_t^{r},
\]

\[
K_i=W_k F_i^{r}, \quad i\in\Omega.
\]

Value 则由归一化特征生成：

\[
V_i=W_v F_i^{n}.
\]

局部窗口内的时序对应关系计算为：

\[
C_{t,i}
=
\text{Softmax}
\left(
\frac{Q_tK_i^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}
\right),
\quad i\in\Omega,
\]

其中，\(d\) 表示每个注意力头的通道维度。对应矩阵 \(C_{t,i}\) 表示中心帧 token 与邻帧 token 之间的时序匹配关系。由于 Query-Key 匹配是在抑制光照后的残差特征上进行的，因此该对应关系对亮度波动和光照闪烁更加鲁棒。

邻帧时序支持可以表示为：

\[
A_{t,i}=C_{t,i}V_i,
\]

多帧支持进一步聚合为：

\[
A_{t,\Omega}
=
\text{Agg}\left(\{A_{t,i}\mid i\in\Omega\}\right),
\]

其中，\(\text{Agg}(\cdot)\) 表示时序聚合操作，可以采用拼接后线性映射或加权求和实现。

---

## 3.2.2 残差校正

虽然邻帧能够提供有用的时序信息，但中心帧仍然应当作为重建过程中的语义锚点。因此，我们引入残差校正机制，将邻帧时序支持与中心帧特征进行结合：

\[
Y_t
=
F_t
+
W_o A_{t,\Omega},
\]

其中，\(W_o\) 表示输出投影层。该残差设计能够保留中心帧结构，同时补充邻帧提供的时序信息，并避免过度时序融合导致的细节平滑问题。

---

## 3.2.3 熵引导源先验生成

时序对应矩阵不仅提供对齐信息，也包含匹配不确定性。当注意力分布集中时，说明时序匹配较为稳定；当注意力分布分散时，说明当前位置可能存在运动模糊、遮挡、错位或严重时序不一致。

我们使用注意力熵度量这种不确定性：

\[
H_t
=
-
\sum_j
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}
\log
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}.
\]

随后，将熵图归一化，并映射为运动源先验：

\[
P_t^{mot}
=
\sigma
\left(
\text{Conv}(H_t)
\right),
\]

其中，\(\sigma(\cdot)\) 表示 Sigmoid 函数。光照源先验通过互补关系得到：

\[
P_t^{illu}
=
1-P_t^{mot}.
\]

直观上，\(P_t^{mot}\) 用于强调时序对应不可靠的区域，例如运动模糊、遮挡和错位区域；\(P_t^{illu}\) 则用于强调更需要进行光照源处理的区域，例如曝光波动和闪烁主导区域。

---

## 3.2.4 源解耦与反向先验投影

根据估计得到的源先验，我们将校正后的中心帧特征 \(Y_t\) 解耦为两个源感知特征：

\[
F_t^{mot}
=
P_t^{mot}
\odot
Y_t,
\]

\[
F_t^{illu}
=
P_t^{illu}
\odot
Y_t,
\]

其中，\(\odot\) 表示逐元素乘法。该软路由方式并不强制进行硬二值分离，而是允许每个空间位置自适应地选择运动源处理和光照源处理的比例。

为了保留多帧源感知推理，我们进一步使用转置时序对应矩阵将中心帧先验反向投影到邻帧：

\[
P_i^{mot}
=
C_{t,i}^{\top}P_t^{mot},
\]

\[
P_i^{illu}
=
C_{t,i}^{\top}P_t^{illu},
\quad i\in\Omega.
\]

这种反向先验投影使邻帧仍然参与源解耦恢复过程，避免过早将所有时序信息压缩到中心帧特征中。

---

## 3.3 光照源处理网络

ISPN 的目标是抑制光照闪烁并稳定跨帧曝光。其核心思想是利用邻帧估计共享光照参考，然后使用该参考对中心帧光照特征进行校正。

给定光照源特征 \(F_t^{illu}\)、邻帧特征 \(F_{\Omega}\) 以及反向投影得到的邻帧光照先验 \(\{P_i^{illu}\}\)，ISPN 首先提取仅来自邻帧的光照证据：

\[
G_{\Omega}^{illu}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{
P_i^{illu}\odot F_i
\mid
i\in\Omega
\}
\right).
\]

随后，网络估计共享光照表示：

\[
L_t^{ref}
=
\mathcal{G}_{illu}(G_{\Omega}^{illu}),
\]

其中，\(\mathcal{G}_{illu}\) 表示轻量级光照估计网络。中心帧光照特征通过下式进行校正：

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}
\left(
F_t^{illu},
L_t^{ref},
P_t^{illu}
\right).
\]

与简单的时序特征融合不同，ISPN 主要利用邻帧估计稳定的光照参考，从而降低帧间亮度波动，同时保留中心帧的结构内容。

---

## 3.4 运动源处理网络

MSPN 负责恢复运动相关退化，包括运动模糊、鬼影和跨帧错位。不同于额外引入光流估计模块，MSPN 直接复用 MINS 生成的时序对应矩阵。

对于每一个邻帧，我们首先将其运动源特征对齐到中心帧坐标系下：

\[
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}^{mot}
=
C_{t,i}
\left(
P_i^{mot}\odot F_i
\right).
\]

随后，对对齐后的邻帧运动特征进行自适应融合：

\[
G_t^{mot}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}^{mot}
\mid
i\in\Omega
\}
\right).
\]

最终，MSPN 恢复运动源特征：

\[
\hat{F}_t^{mot}
=
\mathcal{B}_{mot}
\left(
F_t^{mot},
G_t^{mot},
P_t^{mot}
\right).
\]

由于 MSPN 复用了光照不变的时序对应矩阵，其对齐过程更不容易受到闪烁和曝光变化影响，因此能够在低光视频中实现更加鲁棒的运动恢复。

---

## 3.5 先验引导最终重建

在获得恢复后的光照源特征 \(\hat{F}_t^{illu}\) 和运动源特征 \(\hat{F}_t^{mot}\) 后，我们使用轻量级重建头生成增强后的中心帧。重建过程由源先验进行引导：

\[
\Delta I_t
=
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\hat{F}_t^{illu},
\hat{F}_t^{mot},
P_t^{illu},
P_t^{mot}
\right).
\]

最终增强结果通过残差学习得到：

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
I_t
+
\Delta I_t.
\]

我们有意保持最终重建头的轻量化，因为 MINS-Net 的主要贡献集中在所提出的 Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework 以及源解耦时序推理机制上。

---

## 3.6 训练目标

在第一阶段实现中，我们采用简洁的训练目标，由重建损失和先验正则项组成。给定正常光照下的真实中心帧 \(I_t^{gt}\)，重建损失为：

\[
\mathcal{L}_{rec}
=
\left\|
\hat{I}_t
-
I_t^{gt}
\right\|_1.
\]

为了鼓励先验图具有空间平滑性和物理可解释性，我们对源先验加入总变分正则：

\[
\mathcal{L}_{prior}
=
\left\|
\nabla P_t^{mot}
\right\|_1
+
\left\|
\nabla P_t^{illu}
\right\|_1.
\]

最终训练目标为：

\[
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{rec}
+
\lambda
\mathcal{L}_{prior},
\]

其中，\(\lambda\) 为平衡系数。

## 3. Method

Our **MINS-Net** is based on our formulated **Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework, IFND**. IFND is designed for low-light video enhancement and aims to decouple the mixed degradation in low-light videos into imaging-related corruption and flicker-related temporal inconsistency. Specifically, IFND consists of three key modules: **Motion-Illumination Noise Separation, MINS**, **Illumination-Source Processing Network, ISPN**, and **Motion-Source Processing Network, MSPN**. We design MINS to play the role of the degradation separator. It separates the input multi-frame features into illumination-source features and motion-source features by constructing illumination-invariant temporal correspondence and estimating source-aware priors. Then, ISPN processes the illumination-source features to suppress illumination flicker and exposure fluctuation, while MSPN processes the motion-source features to restore motion-related degradation with the help of the temporal correspondence generated by MINS. Finally, the outputs of ISPN and MSPN are fused to predict the enhanced compensation and reconstruct the final enhanced frame.

The basic unit of MINS-Net is the **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention Block, SD-TMSAB**. It is composed of layer normalization, a local-window illumination-invariant query-key generator, a cross-frame mutual attention module, a residual correction module, an entropy-guided source gate, and a feed-forward network. The core operation in SD-TMSAB is **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention, SD-TMSA**, which differs from conventional temporal mutual self-attention by explicitly suppressing illumination disturbance during temporal matching and producing decoupled motion-source and illumination-source representations.

## 3.1 Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework

Given a low-light video clip centered at the \(t\)-th frame,

\[
\mathcal{I}_t=\{I_{t-k},\ldots,I_{t-1},I_t,I_{t+1},\ldots,I_{t+k}\},
\]

our goal is to restore the enhanced center frame \(\hat{I}_t\). Low-light video degradation is more complicated than single-image degradation because it contains both intra-frame imaging corruption and inter-frame temporal inconsistency. In low-light environments, the captured frames usually suffer from low photon count, shot noise, read noise, color distortion, motion blur, and unstable illumination. Moreover, automatic exposure variation and external light flicker can cause obvious frame-to-frame brightness jumps.

Therefore, we formulate low-light video enhancement as an **imaging-flicker noise decoupling** problem. The degradation can be roughly divided into two components:

\[
\mathcal{D}_{low}=\mathcal{D}_{img}+\mathcal{D}_{flicker},
\]

where \(\mathcal{D}_{img}\) denotes imaging-related corruption, including low-light noise, blur, and detail loss, while \(\mathcal{D}_{flicker}\) denotes temporal illumination fluctuation caused by exposure variation or unstable light sources.

However, directly separating these two degradations in the image space is difficult. In videos, imaging corruption and flicker inconsistency are usually coupled with motion. Therefore, IFND performs degradation decoupling in the feature space through three modules: MINS, ISPN, and MSPN.

The overall pipeline can be formulated as:

\[
F_t,F_{\Omega}=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{I}_t),
\]

\[
F_t^{motion},F_t^{illu},C_{t,\Omega},P_t^{motion},P_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{M}(F_t,F_{\Omega}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}(F_t^{illu},P_t^{illu}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{motion}
=
\mathcal{B}_{motion}(F_t^{motion},F_{\Omega},C_{t,\Omega},P_t^{motion}),
\]

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
\mathcal{R}(\hat{F}_t^{illu},\hat{F}_t^{motion}),
\]

where \(\mathcal{E}\) is the feature extractor, \(F_t\) is the center-frame feature, and \(F_{\Omega}\) denotes the neighboring-frame features. \(\mathcal{M}\) represents the proposed MINS module. \(C_{t,\Omega}\) is the temporal correspondence matrix from the center frame to neighboring frames. \(P_t^{motion}\) and \(P_t^{illu}\) are motion-source and illumination-source priors. \(\mathcal{B}_{illu}\) and \(\mathcal{B}_{motion}\) denote ISPN and MSPN, respectively. \(\mathcal{R}\) is the final reconstruction module.

## 3.2 Motion-Illumination Noise Separation

The proposed **Motion-Illumination Noise Separation, MINS**, is the core component of MINS-Net. Existing temporal attention modules, such as TMSA in video restoration Transformers, usually regard cross-frame differences mainly as motion misalignment. However, this assumption is not always valid in low-light videos. In low-light scenes, a large portion of cross-frame variation comes from illumination jump or flicker rather than pure motion. If temporal attention is directly performed on raw features, the network may confuse illumination variation with motion displacement, resulting in inaccurate temporal matching and unstable enhancement.

To address this problem, MINS introduces **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention, SD-TMSA**. Given the center-frame feature

\[
F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C},
\]

and neighboring-frame features

\[
F_{\Omega}=\{F_i \mid i\in\Omega\},
\]

where

\[
\Omega=\{t-k,\ldots,t-1,t+1,\ldots,t+k\},
\]

MINS outputs:

\[
F_t^{motion},\quad F_t^{illu},\quad C_{t,\Omega},\quad H_t.
\]

Here, \(F_t^{motion}\) is the motion-source feature sent to MSPN, and \(F_t^{illu}\) is the illumination-source feature sent to ISPN. \(C_{t,\Omega}\) denotes the temporal correspondence matrix between the center frame and neighboring frames, which can be further reused by MSPN for temporal alignment. \(H_t\) is the attention entropy map, which measures the uncertainty of temporal matching.

---

## 3.2.1 Local-Window Illumination-Invariant Query-Key Generation

A key issue in low-light video enhancement is that the brightness constancy assumption is often violated. Neighboring frames may share similar structures but have different illumination intensities due to exposure adjustment or flicker. Therefore, using raw features to generate query and key may cause attention matching to be dominated by illumination variation.

Inspired by the Retinex assumption that illumination variation mainly lies in smooth low-frequency components, we construct illumination-invariant tokens for query-key generation. For an input feature \(F\), we first normalize it by layer normalization:

\[
F^n=\text{LN}(F).
\]

Then, a local low-frequency component is extracted by a lightweight local averaging operator \(\phi(\cdot)\). The illumination-suppressed residual feature is obtained as:

\[
F^r=F^n-\phi(F^n).
\]

The query and key are generated only from the residual features:

\[
Q_t=W_qF_t^r,
\]

\[
K_i=W_kF_i^r,\quad i\in\Omega.
\]

The value is generated from the normalized feature:

\[
V_i=W_vF_i^n.
\]

In this way, the query-key matching focuses more on structure consistency and is less affected by frame-to-frame brightness changes.

---

## 3.2.2 Cross-Frame Mutual Attention

After obtaining illumination-invariant query and key tokens, MINS performs cross-frame mutual attention between the center frame and neighboring frames. For each neighboring frame \(i\), the temporal correspondence matrix is computed as:

\[
C_{t,i}
=
\text{Softmax}
\left(
\frac{Q_tK_i^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}
\right),
\]

where \(d\) is the channel dimension of each attention head. \(C_{t,i}\) represents the token-level matching relationship from the center frame to the neighboring frame.

The neighboring-frame feature is then aggregated into the center-frame coordinate space:

\[
A_{t,i}=C_{t,i}V_i.
\]

For all neighboring frames, we obtain:

\[
A_{t,\Omega}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{A_{t,i}\mid i\in\Omega\}
\right),
\]

where \(\text{Agg}(\cdot)\) denotes temporal aggregation, which can be implemented by concatenation followed by linear projection or weighted summation.

---

## 3.2.3 Residual Correction

Although neighboring frames provide useful temporal information, the center frame should remain the semantic anchor of the restored result. Therefore, we introduce a residual correction strategy to preserve the center-frame representation while injecting temporal information from neighboring frames:

\[
Y_t
=
F_t
+
W_oA_{t,\Omega},
\]

where \(W_o\) denotes the output projection layer. This residual design avoids excessive temporal fusion and helps preserve the structure and details of the center frame.

---

## 3.2.4 Entropy-Guided Source Gate

The temporal correspondence matrix contains not only alignment information but also uncertainty information. When attention is concentrated, the temporal matching is reliable. When attention is scattered, the corresponding region may suffer from motion blur, occlusion, misalignment, or complex degradation.

We use attention entropy to estimate the matching uncertainty:

\[
H_t
=
-
\sum_j
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}
\log
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}.
\]

Then, an entropy-guided source gate is used to generate the motion-source prior:

\[
P_t^{motion}
=
\sigma
\left(
\text{Conv}(H_t)
\right),
\]

where \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is the sigmoid function. The illumination-source prior is obtained by the complementary constraint:

\[
P_t^{illu}=1-P_t^{motion}.
\]

The motion-source prior highlights regions with unreliable temporal correspondence, such as motion blur and misalignment. The illumination-source prior emphasizes regions that are more suitable for illumination correction and flicker suppression.

Finally, the corrected feature \(Y_t\) is decoupled into two source-aware features:

\[
F_t^{motion}
=
P_t^{motion}\odot Y_t,
\]

\[
F_t^{illu}
=
P_t^{illu}\odot Y_t,
\]

where \(\odot\) denotes element-wise multiplication. This soft decoupling mechanism allows each spatial location to adaptively select motion-source processing or illumination-source processing.

---

## 3.3 Illumination-Source Processing Network

The **Illumination-Source Processing Network, ISPN**, is designed to process illumination-source degradation. In low-light videos, illumination-source degradation mainly comes from unstable lighting, automatic exposure switching, and frame-to-frame brightness fluctuation. These factors produce flicker artifacts and temporal inconsistency, even when the underlying scene structure remains unchanged.

Given the illumination-source feature \(F_t^{illu}\) and illumination prior \(P_t^{illu}\), ISPN estimates an illumination correction representation:

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}
\left(
F_t^{illu},P_t^{illu}
\right).
\]

The prior \(P_t^{illu}\) guides the network to focus on regions dominated by illumination variation. Instead of treating all degradation uniformly, ISPN specifically learns to stabilize brightness, suppress illumination flicker, and recover under-exposed regions.

In practice, ISPN can be implemented as a lightweight restoration branch composed of convolutional blocks, normalization layers, and attention units. Its output represents the restored illumination-source feature, which will be fused with the motion-source feature for final reconstruction.

---

## 3.4 Motion-Source Processing Network

The **Motion-Source Processing Network, MSPN**, is responsible for processing motion-source degradation. Motion-source degradation includes motion blur, temporal misalignment, ghosting, and structure inconsistency among neighboring frames. Different from methods that rely on explicit optical flow, MSPN reuses the temporal correspondence matrix \(C_{t,\Omega}\) generated by MINS.

For each neighboring frame, MSPN aligns the neighboring feature to the center-frame coordinate space:

\[
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}
=
C_{t,i}F_i.
\]

Then, the aligned neighboring features are aggregated:

\[
G_t^{motion}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}
\mid i\in\Omega
\}
\right).
\]

The motion-source feature is restored by:

\[
\hat{F}_t^{motion}
=
\mathcal{B}_{motion}
\left(
F_t^{motion},
G_t^{motion},
P_t^{motion}
\right).
\]

Since \(C_{t,\Omega}\) is computed using illumination-invariant query-key tokens, MSPN can perform more robust temporal alignment under low-light flicker conditions. This design reduces the influence of brightness variation on motion modeling and improves the restoration of blurred or misaligned regions.

---

## 3.5 Feature Fusion and Final Reconstruction

After ISPN and MSPN produce the restored illumination-source and motion-source features, MINS-Net fuses them to predict the final enhancement compensation:

\[
\Delta I_t
=
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\hat{F}_t^{illu},
\hat{F}_t^{motion}
\right).
\]

The enhanced center frame is obtained by residual reconstruction:

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
I_t
+
\Delta I_t.
\]

This design allows the network to focus on predicting the missing enhancement component rather than directly synthesizing the whole image. The final reconstruction module is kept lightweight, because the main restoration ability comes from the source decoupling of MINS and the branch-specific processing of ISPN and MSPN.

## 3. 方法

本文提出的 **MINS-Net** 基于我们构建的 **Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework，IFND**，即 **成像-闪烁噪声解耦框架**。IFND 面向低光视频增强任务，旨在将低光视频中的混合退化解耦为成像相关退化和闪烁相关时序不一致。具体而言，IFND 主要由三个核心模块组成：**Motion-Illumination Noise Separation，MINS，运动-光源噪声分离模块**，**Illumination-Source Processing Network，ISPN，光照源处理分支**，以及 **Motion-Source Processing Network，MSPN，运动源处理分支**。我们设计 MINS 作为退化源分离模块。MINS 通过构建光照不变的时序对应关系并估计源感知先验，将输入的多帧特征分离为光照源特征和运动源特征。随后，ISPN 对光照源特征进行处理，用于抑制光照闪烁和曝光波动；MSPN 则利用 MINS 生成的时序对应矩阵处理运动源特征，用于恢复运动相关退化。最后，ISPN 和 MSPN 的输出被融合，用于预测增强补偿量并重建最终增强帧。

## 3.1 成像-闪烁噪声解耦框架

给定以第 \(t\) 帧为中心的低光视频片段：

\[
\mathcal{I}_t=\{I_{t-k},\ldots,I_{t-1},I_t,I_{t+1},\ldots,I_{t+k}\},
\]

我们的目标是恢复增强后的中心帧 \(\hat{I}_t\)。与单帧低光图像增强相比，低光视频增强更加复杂，因为它不仅包含帧内成像退化，也包含帧间时序不一致。在低光环境中，视频帧通常受到低光子数量、光子散粒噪声、传感器读出噪声、颜色失真、运动模糊和不稳定光照等因素的影响。此外，自动曝光变化和外部光源闪烁会进一步造成明显的帧间亮度跳变。

因此，我们将低光视频增强建模为一个 **成像-闪烁噪声解耦** 问题。低光视频退化可以粗略划分为两类：

\[
\mathcal{D}_{low}=\mathcal{D}_{img}+\mathcal{D}_{flicker},
\]

其中，\(\mathcal{D}_{img}\) 表示成像相关退化，包括低光噪声、模糊和细节丢失；\(\mathcal{D}_{flicker}\) 表示由曝光变化或不稳定光源引起的时序光照波动。

然而，在图像空间中直接分离这两类退化是困难的。在视频中，成像退化和闪烁不一致通常与运动耦合在一起。因此，IFND 通过 MINS、ISPN 和 MSPN 三个模块在特征空间中进行退化解耦与分支恢复。

整体流程可以表示为：

\[
F_t,F_{\Omega}=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{I}_t),
\]

\[
F_t^{motion},F_t^{illu},C_{t,\Omega},P_t^{motion},P_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{M}(F_t,F_{\Omega}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}(F_t^{illu},P_t^{illu}),
\]

\[
\hat{F}_t^{motion}
=
\mathcal{B}_{motion}(F_t^{motion},F_{\Omega},C_{t,\Omega},P_t^{motion}),
\]

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
\mathcal{R}(\hat{F}_t^{illu},\hat{F}_t^{motion}),
\]

其中，\(\mathcal{E}\) 表示特征提取器，\(F_t\) 表示中心帧特征，\(F_{\Omega}\) 表示邻帧特征集合。\(\mathcal{M}\) 表示提出的 MINS 模块。\(C_{t,\Omega}\) 表示中心帧到邻帧的时序对应矩阵。\(P_t^{motion}\) 和 \(P_t^{illu}\) 分别表示运动源先验和光照源先验。\(\mathcal{B}_{illu}\) 和 \(\mathcal{B}_{motion}\) 分别表示 ISPN 和 MSPN。\(\mathcal{R}\) 表示最终重建模块。

## 3.2 运动-光源噪声分离模块

提出的 **Motion-Illumination Noise Separation，MINS** 是 MINS-Net 的核心组成部分。现有视频恢复 Transformer 中的时序注意力模块通常默认跨帧差异主要来自运动错位。然而，该假设在低光视频中并不完全成立。在低光场景下，相邻帧之间的大量差异实际上来自 illumination jump 或 flicker，而不是纯粹的运动。如果直接在原始特征上进行时序注意力计算，网络容易将光照变化误认为运动位移，从而导致时序匹配不准确和增强结果不稳定。

为了解决这一问题，MINS 引入 **Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention，SD-TMSA**。给定中心帧特征：

\[
F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C},
\]

以及邻帧特征集合：

\[
F_{\Omega}=\{F_i \mid i\in\Omega\},
\]

其中：

\[
\Omega=\{t-k,\ldots,t-1,t+1,\ldots,t+k\},
\]

MINS 输出：

\[
F_t^{motion},\quad F_t^{illu},\quad C_{t,\Omega},\quad H_t.
\]

其中，\(F_t^{motion}\) 表示送入 MSPN 的运动源特征，\(F_t^{illu}\) 表示送入 ISPN 的光照源特征。\(C_{t,\Omega}\) 表示中心帧与邻帧之间的时序对应矩阵，可进一步被 MSPN 复用以进行时序对齐。\(H_t\) 是注意力熵图，用于衡量时序匹配的不确定性。

---

## 3.2.1 局部窗口光照不变 Query-Key 生成

低光视频增强的一个关键难点是亮度恒定假设通常不成立。相邻帧可能具有相似的结构内容，但由于曝光调整或光源闪烁而具有不同的亮度强度。因此，直接使用原始特征生成 Query 和 Key，可能会使注意力匹配受到光照变化的主导。

受 Retinex 思想启发，光照变化通常主要存在于平滑的低频分量中，而结构匹配更依赖于去除低频光照后的残差信息。因此，我们构造光照不变的 Query-Key token。对于输入特征 \(F\)，首先进行层归一化：

\[
F^n=\text{LN}(F).
\]

然后，使用轻量级局部平均算子 \(\phi(\cdot)\) 提取局部低频成分，并得到抑制光照后的残差特征：

\[
F^r=F^n-\phi(F^n).
\]

Query 和 Key 仅由残差特征生成：

\[
Q_t=W_qF_t^r,
\]

\[
K_i=W_kF_i^r,\quad i\in\Omega.
\]

Value 则由归一化特征生成：

\[
V_i=W_vF_i^n.
\]

通过这种方式，Query-Key 匹配更加关注结构一致性，而不容易受到帧间亮度变化的干扰。

---

## 3.2.2 跨帧互注意力

在获得光照不变的 Query 和 Key 后，MINS 在中心帧和邻帧之间执行跨帧互注意力。对于每个邻帧 \(i\)，时序对应矩阵计算为：

\[
C_{t,i}
=
\text{Softmax}
\left(
\frac{Q_tK_i^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}
\right),
\]

其中，\(d\) 表示每个注意力头的通道维度。\(C_{t,i}\) 表示中心帧 token 到邻帧 token 的匹配关系。

随后，将邻帧特征聚合到中心帧坐标空间中：

\[
A_{t,i}=C_{t,i}V_i.
\]

对于所有邻帧，可得到多帧时序支持：

\[
A_{t,\Omega}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{A_{t,i}\mid i\in\Omega\}
\right),
\]

其中，\(\text{Agg}(\cdot)\) 表示时序聚合操作，可以采用拼接后线性映射或加权求和实现。

---

## 3.2.3 残差校正

虽然邻帧能够提供有效的时序信息，但中心帧仍然应当作为最终恢复结果的语义锚点。因此，我们引入残差校正策略，在保留中心帧表示的同时引入邻帧时序信息：

\[
Y_t
=
F_t
+
W_oA_{t,\Omega},
\]

其中，\(W_o\) 表示输出投影层。该残差设计能够避免过度时序融合，同时有助于保留中心帧的结构和细节。

---

## 3.2.4 熵引导源门控

时序对应矩阵不仅提供对齐信息，也包含匹配不确定性信息。当注意力分布集中时，说明时序匹配较为可靠；当注意力分布分散时，对应区域可能存在运动模糊、遮挡、错位或复杂退化。

我们使用注意力熵估计匹配不确定性：

\[
H_t
=
-
\sum_j
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}
\log
C_{t,\Omega}^{j}.
\]

随后，通过熵引导源门控生成运动源先验：

\[
P_t^{motion}
=
\sigma
\left(
\text{Conv}(H_t)
\right),
\]

其中，\(\sigma(\cdot)\) 表示 Sigmoid 函数。光照源先验由互补约束得到：

\[
P_t^{illu}=1-P_t^{motion}.
\]

运动源先验用于强调时序对应不可靠的区域，例如运动模糊和错位区域。光照源先验则用于强调更加适合进行光照校正和闪烁抑制的区域。

最后，校正后的特征 \(Y_t\) 被解耦为两个源感知特征：

\[
F_t^{motion}
=
P_t^{motion}\odot Y_t,
\]

\[
F_t^{illu}
=
P_t^{illu}\odot Y_t,
\]

其中，\(\odot\) 表示逐元素乘法。该软解耦机制允许每个空间位置自适应地选择运动源处理或光照源处理。

---

## 3.3 光照源处理分支

**Illumination-Source Processing Network，ISPN** 用于处理光照源退化。在低光视频中，光照源退化主要来自不稳定光照、自动曝光切换和帧间亮度波动。这些因素即使在场景结构保持不变的情况下，也会产生闪烁伪影和时序不一致。

给定光照源特征 \(F_t^{illu}\) 和光照源先验 \(P_t^{illu}\)，ISPN 估计光照校正表示：

\[
\hat{F}_t^{illu}
=
\mathcal{B}_{illu}
\left(
F_t^{illu},P_t^{illu}
\right).
\]

其中，\(P_t^{illu}\) 引导网络关注由光照变化主导的区域。与统一处理所有退化不同，ISPN 专门学习稳定亮度、抑制光照闪烁并恢复欠曝光区域。

在实现上，ISPN 可以由轻量级卷积块、归一化层和注意力单元构成。其输出表示恢复后的光照源特征，并将在最终重建阶段与运动源特征进行融合。

---

## 3.4 运动源处理分支

**Motion-Source Processing Network，MSPN** 负责处理运动源退化。运动源退化包括运动模糊、跨帧错位、鬼影以及邻帧之间的结构不一致。不同于依赖显式光流估计的方法，MSPN 直接复用 MINS 生成的时序对应矩阵 \(C_{t,\Omega}\)。

对于每个邻帧，MSPN 首先将其特征对齐到中心帧坐标空间：

\[
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}
=
C_{t,i}F_i.
\]

随后，对齐后的邻帧特征被聚合为运动时序支持：

\[
G_t^{motion}
=
\text{Agg}
\left(
\{
\tilde{F}_{i\rightarrow t}
\mid i\in\Omega
\}
\right).
\]

运动源特征进一步通过 MSPN 恢复：

\[
\hat{F}_t^{motion}
=
\mathcal{B}_{motion}
\left(
F_t^{motion},
G_t^{motion},
P_t^{motion}
\right).
\]

由于 \(C_{t,\Omega}\) 是通过光照不变 Query-Key token 计算得到的，因此 MSPN 在低光闪烁场景下能够实现更加鲁棒的时序对齐。该设计降低了亮度变化对运动建模的影响，有助于恢复模糊或错位区域。

---

## 3.5 特征融合与最终重建

在 ISPN 和 MSPN 分别得到恢复后的光照源特征和运动源特征后，MINS-Net 对两类特征进行融合，并预测最终增强补偿量：

\[
\Delta I_t
=
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\hat{F}_t^{illu},
\hat{F}_t^{motion}
\right).
\]

最终增强后的中心帧通过残差重建得到：

\[
\hat{I}_t
=
I_t
+
\Delta I_t.
\]

这种设计使网络专注于预测低光输入与正常光输出之间缺失的增强补偿，而不是直接从零合成完整图像。最终重建模块保持轻量化，因为 MINS-Net 的主要恢复能力来自 MINS 的源解耦机制以及 ISPN、MSPN 的分支特定处理。

# MINS-Net: Motion-Illumination Noise Separation for Low-Light Video Enhancement

## Abstract

Low-light video enhancement remains challenging because the degradation is not caused by a single factor. In real videos, severe under-exposure is usually entangled with temporal illumination fluctuation, sensor noise, motion blur, and cross-frame misalignment. Existing low-light image enhancement methods mainly improve spatial visibility frame by frame, while existing video restoration approaches often rely on brightness constancy or explicit motion alignment, which becomes unreliable under abrupt exposure changes and flicker. In this work, we propose MINS-Net, a Motion-Illumination Noise Separation network for low-light video enhancement. The core idea is to reformulate low-light video enhancement as a source-decoupled restoration problem and explicitly separate illumination-related degradation from motion-related degradation before reconstruction. To this end, we introduce a Motion-Illumination Noise Separation (MINS) module that builds illumination-invariant temporal correspondence within local windows, derives center-guided source priors from attention entropy, and projects the priors back to neighboring frames to preserve multi-frame reasoning. On top of MINS, an Illumination-Source Processing Network (ISPN) aggregates neighbor-only illumination evidence to suppress flicker and stabilize cross-frame exposure, while a Motion-Source Processing Network (MSPN) reuses the correspondence matrix to align motion features and adaptively fuse dynamic support under the center-frame coordinate system. Finally, a lightweight prior-guided reconstruction head combines the two restored branches into the enhanced frame. The resulting framework is physically interpretable, temporally aware, and well aligned with the characteristics of low-light video degradation.

---

## 1. Introduction

Low-light video enhancement is an important problem in computational photography, mobile imaging, and intelligent perception. Compared with low-light image enhancement, the video setting introduces an additional temporal dimension that is both a source of information and a source of difficulty. Neighboring frames provide redundant structure and context that can help recover details hidden in darkness, yet low-light videos also exhibit frame-to-frame brightness fluctuation, motion blur, sensor noise, rolling artifacts, and correspondence ambiguity.

Single-image low-light enhancement methods, especially Retinex-based models, have shown strong ability to improve visibility by reasoning about illumination and reflectance. However, frame-wise application often produces temporal inconsistency. On the other hand, transformer-based video restoration methods such as VRT demonstrate the effectiveness of local spatio-temporal attention, but they are not explicitly designed for the illumination instability that dominates low-light videos.

We argue that low-light video enhancement should be reformulated as a source-decoupled restoration problem. In particular, illumination-source degradation and motion-source degradation behave differently across time and should not be mixed too early. Illumination changes mainly corrupt exposure consistency and invalidate brightness constancy, while motion degradation requires reliable temporal support and alignment.

Based on this observation, we propose MINS-Net, whose core is the Motion-Illumination Noise Separation (MINS) module. MINS first constructs illumination-invariant temporal correspondence, then derives source priors from correspondence entropy, and finally projects the center-frame priors back to neighboring frames to preserve multi-frame source-aware reasoning. The downstream restoration is divided into ISPN for illumination correction and MSPN for motion restoration, followed by a lightweight prior-guided reconstruction head.

Our formulation offers a coherent narrative with clear physical interpretation and implementation feasibility. The main contributions are three-fold: first, we reformulate low-light video enhancement as a source-decoupled restoration problem; second, we design MINS for illumination-invariant correspondence learning, entropy-guided prior estimation, and backward prior projection; third, we build branch-specific restoration modules that turn these priors into temporally consistent final enhancement.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Overall Framework

The overall architecture of our proposed dual-stage approach is illustrated in Fig. 1, our MINS-Net is built upon the proposed Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling (IFND) Framework for low-light video enhancement. The proposed IFND is composed of three main parts: Motion-Illumination Noise Separation (MINS), Illumination-Source Processing Network (ISPN), and Motion-Source Processing Network (MSPN). Specifically, MINS separates the mixed degradation into source-aware features, which are subsequently refined by ISPN and MSPN for final reconstruction.

The basic unit of MINS-Net is the Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention Block, SD-TMSAB. It is composed of layer normalization, a local-window illumination-invariant query-key generator, a cross-frame mutual attention module, a residual correction module, an entropy-guided source gate, and a feed-forward network. The core operation in SD-TMSAB is Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention, SD-TMSA, which differs from conventional temporal mutual self-attention by explicitly suppressing illumination disturbance during temporal matching and producing decoupled motion-source and illumination-source representations.

---

### 2.2 Problem Formulation

Given a sliding temporal window centered at time \( t \), denoted as:

```latex
\{I_{t-k}, \ldots, I_t, \ldots, I_{t+k}\}
```

our goal is to estimate an enhanced center frame:

```latex
\hat{I}_t
```

that is both visually bright and temporally consistent. Instead of regarding the task as a single black-box restoration mapping, we explicitly model two degradation sources: an illumination source related to under-exposure and cross-frame fluctuation, and a motion source related to blur, ghosting, and correspondence ambiguity.

---

### 2.3 Architecture Overview

The proposed pipeline contains four stages: a shared pyramid feature extractor, the Motion-Illumination Noise Separation module, two source-specific restoration branches, and a prior-guided final reconstruction head. A shared encoder first maps the input frames into a center-frame feature and neighboring features. MINS then estimates illumination-invariant temporal correspondence, source priors, and source-decoupled features. ISPN restores illumination-related content, MSPN restores motion-related content, and the final head reconstructs the enhanced frame under the guidance of the source priors.

The overall pipeline can be formulated as:

```latex
F_t = E(I_t), \qquad F_{\Omega} = \{F_i = E(I_i) \mid i \in \Omega\}
```

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, C_{t,\Omega}, P_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = M(F_t, F_{\Omega})
```

```latex
\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = B_{\mathrm{illu}}(F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, P_t^{\mathrm{illu}})
```

```latex
\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = B_{\mathrm{motion}}(F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, F_{\Omega}, C_{t,\Omega}, P_t^{\mathrm{motion}})
```

```latex
\Delta I_t = R(\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, \hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{motion}})
```

```latex
\hat{I}_t = I_t + \Delta I_t
```

where \( E \) is the feature extractor, \( F_t \) is the center-frame feature, and \( F_{\Omega} \) denotes the neighboring-frame features. \( M \) represents the proposed MINS module. \( C_{t,\Omega} \) is the temporal correspondence matrix from the center frame to neighboring frames. \( P_t^{\mathrm{motion}} \) and \( P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \) are motion-source and illumination-source priors. \( B_{\mathrm{illu}} \) and \( B_{\mathrm{motion}} \) denote ISPN and MSPN, respectively. \( R \) is the final reconstruction module.

---

### 2.4 Motion-Illumination Noise Separation

The proposed Motion-Illumination Noise Separation, MINS, is the core component of MINS-Net. Existing temporal attention modules, such as TMSA in video restoration Transformers, usually regard cross-frame differences mainly as motion misalignment. However, this assumption is not always valid in low-light videos. In low-light scenes, a large portion of cross-frame variation comes from illumination jump or flicker rather than pure motion. If temporal attention is directly performed on raw features, the network may confuse illumination variation with motion displacement, resulting in inaccurate temporal matching and unstable enhancement.

To address this problem, MINS introduces Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention, SD-TMSA. Given the center-frame feature:

```latex
F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}
```

and neighboring-frame features:

```latex
F_{\Omega} = \{F_i \mid i \in \Omega\}
```

where:

```latex
\Omega = \{t-k, \ldots, t-1, t+1, \ldots, t+k\}
```

MINS outputs:

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, C_{t,\Omega}, H_t
```

Here, \( F_t^{\mathrm{motion}} \) is the motion-source feature sent to MSPN, and \( F_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \) is the illumination-source feature sent to ISPN. \( C_{t,\Omega} \) denotes the temporal correspondence matrix between the center frame and neighboring frames, which can be further reused by MSPN for temporal alignment. \( H_t \) is the attention entropy map, which measures the uncertainty of temporal matching.

---

### 2.4.1 Local-Window Illumination-Invariant Query-Key Generation

A key issue in low-light video enhancement is that the brightness constancy assumption is often violated. Neighboring frames may share similar structures but have different illumination intensities due to exposure adjustment or flicker. Therefore, using raw features to generate query and key may cause attention matching to be dominated by illumination variation.

Inspired by the Retinex assumption that illumination variation mainly lies in smooth low-frequency components, we construct illumination-invariant tokens for query-key generation. For an input feature \( F \), we first normalize it by layer normalization:

```latex
F^n = \mathrm{LN}(F)
```

Then, a local low-frequency component is extracted by a lightweight local averaging operator \( \phi(\cdot) \). The illumination-suppressed residual feature is obtained as:

```latex
F^r = F^n - \phi(F^n)
```

The query and key are generated only from the residual features:

```latex
Q_t = W_q F_t^r
```

```latex
K_i = W_k F_i^r, \qquad i \in \Omega
```

The value is generated from the normalized feature:

```latex
V_i = W_v F_i^n
```

In this way, the query-key matching focuses more on structure consistency and is less affected by frame-to-frame brightness changes.

---

### 2.4.2 Cross-Frame Mutual Attention

After obtaining illumination-invariant query and key tokens, MINS performs cross-frame mutual attention between the center frame and neighboring frames. For each neighboring frame \( i \), the temporal correspondence matrix is computed as:

```latex
C_{t,i} = \mathrm{Softmax}\left(\frac{Q_t K_i^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)
```

where \( d \) is the channel dimension of each attention head. \( C_{t,i} \) represents the token-level matching relationship from the center frame to the neighboring frame.

The neighboring-frame feature is then aggregated into the center-frame coordinate space:

```latex
A_{t,i} = C_{t,i} V_i
```

For all neighboring frames, we obtain:

```latex
A_{t,\Omega} = \mathrm{Agg}\left(\{A_{t,i} \mid i \in \Omega\}\right)
```

where \( \mathrm{Agg}(\cdot) \) denotes temporal aggregation, which can be implemented by concatenation followed by linear projection or weighted summation.

---

### 2.4.3 Residual Correction

Although neighboring frames provide useful temporal information, the center frame should remain the semantic anchor of the restored result. Therefore, we introduce a residual correction strategy to preserve the center-frame representation while injecting temporal information from neighboring frames:

```latex
Y_t = F_t + W_o A_{t,\Omega}
```

where \( W_o \) denotes the output projection layer. This residual design avoids excessive temporal fusion and helps preserve the structure and details of the center frame.

---

### 2.4.4 Entropy-Guided Source Gate

The temporal correspondence matrix contains not only alignment information but also uncertainty information. When attention is concentrated, the temporal matching is reliable. When attention is scattered, the corresponding region may suffer from motion blur, occlusion, misalignment, or complex degradation.

We use attention entropy to estimate the matching uncertainty:

```latex
H_t = -\sum_j C_{t,\Omega}^{(j)} \log\left(C_{t,\Omega}^{(j)} + \epsilon\right)
```

Then, an entropy-guided source gate is used to generate the motion-source prior:

```latex
P_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = \sigma(\mathrm{Conv}(H_t))
```

where \( \sigma(\cdot) \) is the sigmoid function. The illumination-source prior is obtained by the complementary constraint:

```latex
P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = 1 - P_t^{\mathrm{motion}}
```

The motion-source prior highlights regions with unreliable temporal correspondence, such as motion blur and misalignment. The illumination-source prior emphasizes regions that are more suitable for illumination correction and flicker suppression.

Finally, the corrected feature \( Y_t \) is decoupled into two source-aware features:

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = P_t^{\mathrm{motion}} \odot Y_t
```

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \odot Y_t
```

where \( \odot \) denotes element-wise multiplication. This soft decoupling mechanism allows each spatial location to adaptively select motion-source processing or illumination-source processing.

---

### 2.5 Illumination-Source Processing Network

The Illumination-Source Processing Network, ISPN, is designed to process illumination-source degradation. In low-light videos, illumination-source degradation mainly comes from unstable lighting, automatic exposure switching, and frame-to-frame brightness fluctuation. These factors produce flicker artifacts and temporal inconsistency, even when the underlying scene structure remains unchanged.

Given the illumination-source feature \( F_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \) and illumination prior \( P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \), ISPN estimates an illumination correction representation:

```latex
\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = B_{\mathrm{illu}}(F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, P_t^{\mathrm{illu}})
```

The prior \( P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \) guides the network to focus on regions dominated by illumination variation. Instead of treating all degradation uniformly, ISPN specifically learns to stabilize brightness, suppress illumination flicker, and recover under-exposed regions.

In practice, ISPN can be implemented as a lightweight restoration branch composed of convolutional blocks, normalization layers, and attention units. Its output represents the restored illumination-source feature, which will be fused with the motion-source feature for final reconstruction.

---

### 2.6 Motion-Source Processing Network

The Motion-Source Processing Network, MSPN, is responsible for processing motion-source degradation. Motion-source degradation includes motion blur, temporal misalignment, ghosting, and structure inconsistency among neighboring frames. Different from methods that rely on explicit optical flow, MSPN reuses the temporal correspondence matrix \( C_{t,\Omega} \) generated by MINS.

For each neighboring frame, MSPN aligns the neighboring feature to the center-frame coordinate space:

```latex
\tilde{F}_{i \rightarrow t} = C_{t,i} F_i
```

Then, the aligned neighboring features are aggregated:

```latex
G_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = \mathrm{Agg}\left(\{\tilde{F}_{i \rightarrow t} \mid i \in \Omega\}\right)
```

The motion-source feature is restored by:

```latex
\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = B_{\mathrm{motion}}(F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, G_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, P_t^{\mathrm{motion}})
```

Since \( C_{t,\Omega} \) is computed using illumination-invariant query-key tokens, MSPN can perform more robust temporal alignment under low-light flicker conditions. This design reduces the influence of brightness variation on motion modeling and improves the restoration of blurred or misaligned regions.

---

### 2.7 Final Reconstruction

After ISPN and MSPN produce the restored illumination-source and motion-source features, MINS-Net fuses them to predict the final enhancement compensation:

```latex
\Delta I_t = R(\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, \hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{motion}})
```

The enhanced center frame is obtained by residual reconstruction:

```latex
\hat{I}_t = I_t + \Delta I_t
```

This design allows the network to focus on predicting the missing enhancement component rather than directly synthesizing the whole image. The final reconstruction module is kept lightweight, because the main restoration ability comes from the source decoupling of MINS and the branch-specific processing of ISPN and MSPN.

---

### 2.8 Training Objective

For the first implementation stage, we use a concise objective that balances image-domain reconstruction quality and prior smoothness.

```latex
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{prior}}
```

where \( \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} \) denotes the reconstruction loss and \( \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{prior}} \) denotes the regularization term on the learned priors.

A typical choice is:

```latex
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} = \lVert \hat{I}_t - I_t^{\mathrm{gt}} \rVert_1
```

```latex
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{prior}} = \lVert \nabla P_t^{\mathrm{motion}} \rVert_1 + \lVert \nabla P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \rVert_1
```

where \( I_t^{\mathrm{gt}} \) is the ground-truth enhanced frame and \( \nabla \) denotes spatial gradient.

## Abstract

Low-light video enhancement is challenging because degradation in real videos is not caused by a single factor. Underexposure, temporal illumination fluctuation, sensor noise, motion blur, ghosting, and frame misalignment are often coupled together, making it difficult to achieve both visual clarity and temporal consistency. To address this problem, we propose MINS-Net, a dual-stage framework for low-light video enhancement based on Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling, IFND. The proposed framework explicitly decomposes the mixed degradation into source-aware representations and processes them with two dedicated branches for illumination and motion restoration. Finally, a lightweight reconstruction head fuses the two restored branches to generate the enhanced frame. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed method is effective in restoring details and improving temporal coherence in low-light videos.

## 1. Introduction

Low-light video enhancement has attracted increasing attention in computational photography and video restoration. Compared with low-light image enhancement, the video setting introduces temporal redundancy that can help recover details, but also introduces additional challenges such as brightness fluctuation, motion blur, ghosting, and temporal inconsistency. In real-world low-light videos, the degradation is usually not limited to one factor. Instead, imaging corruption and flicker variation are entangled across frames, making direct enhancement difficult.

Existing methods can be broadly divided into image-based enhancement methods and video restoration methods. Image-based approaches often improve brightness and visibility effectively, but when applied frame by frame they usually suffer from temporal flickering. Video restoration methods exploit temporal information, but many of them implicitly assume brightness constancy or rely on accurate motion alignment, which becomes unreliable under low-light illumination fluctuation.

To address these issues, we propose MINS-Net, a dual-stage low-light video enhancement network built upon the Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling, IFND, framework. The core idea is to explicitly separate degradation into motion-related and illumination-related sources before restoration. In this way, the network can process different degradation types with source-specific branches, leading to more stable enhancement and better temporal consistency.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

1. We formulate low-light video enhancement as an Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling problem and propose a dual-stage restoration framework.
2. We design a Motion-Illumination Noise Separation, MINS, module that performs illumination-invariant temporal matching and entropy-guided source decoupling.
3. We propose two source-specific branches, ISPN and MSPN, to handle illumination degradation and motion degradation separately.
4. We introduce a lightweight reconstruction head that fuses source-aware features to produce the final enhanced frame.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Overall Framework

The overall architecture of our proposed dual-stage approach is illustrated in Fig. 1, our MINS-Net is built upon the proposed Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling (IFND) Framework for low-light video enhancement. The proposed IFND is composed of three main parts: Motion-Illumination Noise Separation (MINS), Illumination-Source Processing Network (ISPN), and Motion-Source Processing Network (MSPN). Specifically, MINS separates the mixed degradation into source-aware features, which are subsequently refined by ISPN and MSPN for final reconstruction.  
The basic unit of MINS-Net is the Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention Block, SD-TMSAB. It is composed of layer normalization, a local-window illumination-invariant query-key generator, a cross-frame mutual attention module, a residual correction module, an entropy-guided source gate, and a feed-forward network. The core operation in SD-TMSAB is Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention, SD-TMSA, which differs from conventional temporal mutual self-attention by explicitly suppressing illumination disturbance during temporal matching and producing decoupled motion-source and illumination-source representations.

---

### 2.2 Imaging-Flicker Noise Decoupling Framework

Given a low-light video clip centered at the \(t\)-th frame, the input can be written as:

```latex
\mathcal{I}_t = \{I_{t-k}, \ldots, I_{t-1}, I_t, I_{t+1}, \ldots, I_{t+k}\}
```

where \(I_t\) denotes the center frame to be enhanced, and the remaining frames serve as temporal neighbors. Our goal is to restore the enhanced center frame:

```latex
\hat{I}_t
```

Low-light video degradation is more complicated than single-image degradation because it contains both intra-frame imaging corruption and inter-frame temporal inconsistency. In low-light environments, captured frames typically suffer from low photon count, shot noise, read noise, color distortion, motion blur, and unstable illumination. Moreover, automatic exposure variation and external light flicker can cause obvious frame-to-frame brightness jumps.

Therefore, we formulate low-light video enhancement as an imaging-flicker noise decoupling problem. The degradation can be roughly divided into two components:

```latex
D_t = D_t^{\mathrm{img}} + D_t^{\mathrm{flicker}}
```

where \(D_t^{\mathrm{img}}\) denotes imaging-related corruption, including low-light noise, blur, and detail loss, while \(D_t^{\mathrm{flicker}}\) denotes temporal illumination fluctuation caused by exposure variation or unstable light sources.

However, directly separating these two degradations in the image space is difficult. In videos, imaging corruption and flicker inconsistency are usually coupled with motion. Therefore, IFND performs degradation decoupling in the feature space through three modules: MINS, ISPN, and MSPN.

The overall pipeline can be formulated as:

```latex
F_t = \mathcal{E}(I_t), \qquad F_{\Omega} = \{F_i = \mathcal{E}(I_i) \mid i \in \Omega\}
```

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, C_{t,\Omega}, P_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = \mathcal{M}(F_t, F_{\Omega})
```

```latex
\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{illu}}(F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, P_t^{\mathrm{illu}})
```

```latex
\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{motion}}(F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, F_{\Omega}, C_{t,\Omega}, P_t^{\mathrm{motion}})
```

```latex
\Delta I_t = \mathcal{R}(\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, \hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{motion}})
```

```latex
\hat{I}_t = I_t + \Delta I_t
```

where \(\mathcal{E}\) is the feature extractor, \(F_t\) is the center-frame feature, and \(F_{\Omega}\) denotes the neighboring-frame features. \(\mathcal{M}\) represents the proposed MINS module. \(C_{t,\Omega}\) is the temporal correspondence matrix from the center frame to neighboring frames. \(P_t^{\mathrm{motion}}\) and \(P_t^{\mathrm{illu}}\) are motion-source and illumination-source priors. \(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{illu}}\) and \(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{motion}}\) denote ISPN and MSPN, respectively. \(\mathcal{R}\) is the final reconstruction module.

### 2.3 Motion-Illumination Noise Separation

The proposed Motion-Illumination Noise Separation, MINS, is the core component of MINS-Net. Existing temporal attention modules, such as TMSA in video restoration Transformers, usually regard cross-frame differences mainly as motion misalignment. However, this assumption is not always valid in low-light videos. In low-light scenes, a large portion of cross-frame variation comes from illumination jump or flicker rather than pure motion. If temporal attention is directly performed on raw features, the network may confuse illumination variation with motion displacement, resulting in inaccurate temporal matching and unstable enhancement.

To address this problem, MINS introduces Source-Decoupled Temporal Mutual Self-Attention, SD-TMSA. Given the center-frame feature:

```latex
F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}
```

and neighboring-frame features:

```latex
F_{\Omega} = \{F_i \mid i \in \Omega\}
```

where:

```latex
\Omega = \{t-k, \ldots, t-1, t+1, \ldots, t+k\}
```

MINS outputs:

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}, F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, C_{t,\Omega}, H_t
```

Here, \(F_t^{\mathrm{motion}}\) is the motion-source feature sent to MSPN, and \(F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}\) is the illumination-source feature sent to ISPN. \(C_{t,\Omega}\) denotes the temporal correspondence matrix between the center frame and neighboring frames, which can be further reused by MSPN for temporal alignment. \(H_t\) is the attention entropy map, which measures the uncertainty of temporal matching.

---

### 2.3.1 Local-Window Illumination-Invariant Query-Key Generation

A key issue in low-light video enhancement is that the brightness constancy assumption is often violated. Neighboring frames may share similar structures but have different illumination intensities due to exposure adjustment or flicker. Therefore, using raw features to generate query and key may cause attention matching to be dominated by illumination variation.

Inspired by the Retinex assumption that illumination variation mainly lies in smooth low-frequency components, we construct illumination-invariant tokens for query-key generation. For an input feature \(F\), we first normalize it by layer normalization:

```latex
F^n = \mathrm{LN}(F)
```

Then, a local low-frequency component is extracted by a lightweight local averaging operator \(\phi(\cdot)\). The illumination-suppressed residual feature is obtained as:

```latex
F^r = F^n - \phi(F^n)
```

The query and key are generated only from the residual features:

```latex
Q_t = W_q F_t^r
```

```latex
K_i = W_k F_i^r, \qquad i \in \Omega
```

The value is generated from the normalized feature:

```latex
V_i = W_v F_i^n
```

In this way, the query-key matching focuses more on structure consistency and is less affected by frame-to-frame brightness changes.

---

### 2.3.2 Cross-Frame Mutual Attention

After obtaining illumination-invariant query and key tokens, MINS performs cross-frame mutual attention between the center frame and neighboring frames. For each neighboring frame \(i\), the temporal correspondence matrix is computed as:

```latex
C_{t,i} = \mathrm{Softmax}\left(\frac{Q_t K_i^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)
```

where \(d\) is the channel dimension of each attention head. \(C_{t,i}\) represents the token-level matching relationship from the center frame to the neighboring frame.

The neighboring-frame feature is then aggregated into the center-frame coordinate space:

```latex
A_{t,i} = C_{t,i} V_i
```

For all neighboring frames, we obtain:

```latex
A_{t,\Omega} = \mathrm{Agg}\left(\{A_{t,i} \mid i \in \Omega\}\right)
```

where \(\mathrm{Agg}(\cdot)\) denotes temporal aggregation, which can be implemented by concatenation followed by linear projection or weighted summation.

---

### 2.3.3 Residual Correction

Although neighboring frames provide useful temporal information, the center frame should remain the semantic anchor of the restored result. Therefore, we introduce a residual correction strategy to preserve the center-frame representation while injecting temporal information from neighboring frames:

```latex
Y_t = F_t + W_o A_{t,\Omega}
```

where \(W_o\) denotes the output projection layer. This residual design avoids excessive temporal fusion and helps preserve the structure and details of the center frame.

---

### 2.3.4 Entropy-Guided Source Gate

The temporal correspondence matrix contains not only alignment information but also uncertainty information. When attention is concentrated, the temporal matching is reliable. When attention is scattered, the corresponding region may suffer from motion blur, occlusion, misalignment, or complex degradation.

We use attention entropy to estimate the matching uncertainty:

```latex
H_t = -\sum_j C_{t,\Omega}^{(j)} \log\left(C_{t,\Omega}^{(j)} + \epsilon\right)
```

Then, an entropy-guided source gate is used to generate the motion-source prior:

```latex
P_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = \sigma(\mathrm{Conv}(H_t))
```

where \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is the sigmoid function. The illumination-source prior is obtained by the complementary constraint:

```latex
P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = 1 - P_t^{\mathrm{motion}}
```

The motion-source prior highlights regions with unreliable temporal correspondence, such as motion blur and misalignment. The illumination-source prior emphasizes regions that are more suitable for illumination correction and flicker suppression.

Finally, the corrected feature \(Y_t\) is decoupled into two source-aware features:

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{motion}} = P_t^{\mathrm{motion}} \odot Y_t
```

```latex
F_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = P_t^{\mathrm{illu}} \odot Y_t
```

where \(\odot\) denotes element-wise multiplication. This soft decoupling mechanism allows each spatial location to adaptively select motion-source processing or illumination-source processing.

---

### 2.4 Illumination-Source Processing Network

The Illumination-Source Processing Network, ISPN, is designed to process illumination-source degradation. In low-light videos, illumination-source degradation mainly comes from unstable lighting, automatic exposure switching, and frame-to-frame brightness fluctuation. These factors produce flicker artifacts and temporal inconsistency, even when the underlying scene structure remains unchanged.

Given the illumination-source feature \(F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}\) and illumination prior \(P_t^{\mathrm{illu}}\), ISPN estimates an illumination correction representation:

```latex
\hat{F}_t^{\mathrm{illu}} = \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{illu}}(F_t^{\mathrm{illu}}, P_t^{\mathrm{illu}})
```

The prior \(P_t^{\mathrm{illu}}\) guides the network to focus on regions dominated by illumination variation. Instead of treating all degradation uniformly, ISPN specifically learns to stabilize brightness, suppress illumination flicker, and recover under-exposed regions.

In practice, ISPN can be implemented as a lightweight restoration branch composed of convolutional blocks, normalization layers, and attention units. Its output represents the restored illumination-source feature, which will be fused with the motion-source feature for final reconstruction.

---
